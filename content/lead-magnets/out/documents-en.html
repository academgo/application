Documents and legalisation: how it works in 9 study destinations

Apostille, consular legalisation and school-certificate recognition — what your destination actually asks for

academgo.com · Warsaw, ul. Złota 7/28

Most failed applications are lost on paperwork, not on grades or money. Apostille, translation and recognition of a school certificate take anywhere from two weeks to three months — and in some countries they cannot be done after you arrive.

This checklist shows what to do at home, before you fly.

The basic pack, needed everywhere

  • A passport valid well beyond the end of your programme.
  • School certificate and transcript (for master's — degree and transcript).
  • Certified translation into the local language or into English.
  • English test certificate, if the programme requires one.
  • Photos to local specification, health insurance, proof of funds.

One rule matters more than the rest: translations are made from the legalised document, not before legalisation. Change the order and you pay for everything twice.

North Cyprus: the honest part about the degree

The TRNC is recognised only by Türkiye. A degree from a North Cyprus university is not recognised in Europe directly. There is one working route: the programme must be listed as YÖK-accredited, and the degree is then legalised through Turkish authorities — sworn translation, notary and an apostille from the competent Turkish body.

Programmes outside the YÖK list are not considered for recognition at all. Check this before you pay a deposit, not after graduation.

Timing: count backwards from your start date

  • Apostille: a few days to three weeks, depending on the issuing body.
  • Consular legalisation (UAE): longer than an apostille — four authorities in sequence.
  • Certificate recognition (Denklik, homologación, CIMEA/DOV, NCEQE): two weeks to several months.
  • Visa or entry permit: allow about a month of official processing plus the appointment queue.

Rule of thumb: start documents four to six months before the semester begins, and start legalisation together with your application — not after the offer arrives.

Three mistakes that cost a year

  • Translating before legalising. The translation must be made from the legalised document.
  • Leaving home without an apostille. In several countries it can only be obtained in person or by proxy — from abroad it is slower and more expensive.
  • Paying the deposit before checking recognition. Deposits are almost always non-refundable.
