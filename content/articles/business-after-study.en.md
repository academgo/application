# Working for Yourself After Graduation Abroad: Residence Permits, Business and Online Presence

Your diploma does not give you the right to work for yourself. Your residence status does.

Graduates usually learn this in the wrong order: they pick a service, name a price, and only then find out that their permit does not cover self-employment. This guide goes in the right order — status first, then the business, then the part most people skip: being findable.

Rules change every year. Check anything that applies to your case with the migration authority of the country and the current tax code.

## Post-Study Work Visa and Residence Permits: What Lets Graduates Work for Themselves

A student visa gets you in. A residence permit decides what you may do once you are there, and the answer differs from country to country.

Poland is one of the more open options. A foreigner holding a residence card issued for full-time studies may register a sole proprietorship. After graduation there is a separate route: a temporary residence permit for graduates of Polish universities, granted for nine months and only once. You apply right after finishing your degree, in person, during a legal stay, and you prove health insurance, accommodation and funds. Starting a business counts as a valid purpose for that permit.

Nine months is a deadline, not a cushion. It is the window in which you register, find the first clients and move to a status that lasts longer.

Elsewhere the logic differs. Some countries treat study permits as study-only and require a separate basis for self-employment. In the Gulf, your status depends on a sponsor — a university, an employer, or a company you own.

Comparing routes country by country is faster than reading five government sites in parallel: this [residence permits and relocation guide for Europe](https://www.moveandinvest.com/) sets out the options for Portugal, Greece, Malta and the UAE, with conditions and timelines side by side.

Whatever route you take, write down two dates: when your current permit expires, and the last day you can apply for the next one. Everything else depends on those.

## UAE Residence Visa and Company Formation in Dubai: the Fastest Route for Graduates

The UAE inverts the usual order. You do not get a visa and then start working — you get a licence, and residency follows from it.

A freelance permit in one of the free zones comes with a renewable two-year residence visa. You cannot hire staff on it, and serving mainland clients from a mainland office needs an extra permit. Costs vary widely between zones, so compare the full first-year total — licence, visa, Emirates ID and insurance — rather than the headline price.

Graduates with a degree have a second option: Green Residency, valid for five years. It asks for an apostilled diploma and proof of income over the previous two years, which rules it out for most people in their first year of self-employment.

Two practical notes. Your licence activity list defines what you may legally invoice for, so choose it wider than your current service. And a free zone is a legal address, not a marketing choice — clients rarely ask which zone you are in.

## Residence Permits in Portugal, Greece and Malta Compared for Self-Employment

Southern Europe offers slower but cheaper routes than the Gulf, and they differ in what they actually allow.

Portugal attracts remote workers and freelancers, and its permits are the most commonly discussed among graduates who want to keep foreign clients. Greece is usually chosen by people who combine a residence route with property. Malta is small, English-speaking and administratively quick, which matters when your whole business is one person and a laptop.

Golden visa routes sit in a different category. They are investment programmes, not graduate routes: the sums involved put them out of reach at this stage, and several countries have narrowed or closed them in recent years.

Before you commit to a country, answer three questions in writing. May you invoice clients outside that country? Does the permit count toward long-term residence later? What happens to your status if your income drops for six months? If a route fails any of them, it is a holiday plan, not a business plan.

## Generative Engine Optimization: How Your Business Appears in ChatGPT and AI Overviews

A growing share of clients never sees a list of ten blue links. They ask an assistant and get one recommendation with a couple of sources.

Getting into that answer is a separate discipline, called generative engine optimization. The principle is simple: AI systems quote what they can verify. They need to find clear facts about your company — what you do, where you work, what it costs, what makes you different from the next provider.

[Generative engine optimization](https://www.bandziuk.com/services/ai-ready-seo-and-geo-optimization) works on three layers: structured data on your own site, mentions on third-party sources the systems trust, and wording specific enough to be quoted without guesswork. Vague lines about a personal approach and flexible terms never survive that filter. Numbers, cities, services and conditions do.

Test it yourself. Ask an assistant the question your client would ask — "who does X in Y city" — and see who comes up. If your name is missing entirely, that is the gap to work on, and it is a different gap from search rankings.

## First-Month Checklist for a New Business Abroad

- Confirm that your residence status allows the activity, and keep the document that proves it.
- Register the company and choose the tax regime after talking to an accountant, not before.
- Open a separate business account.
- Publish a one-page site: service, price range, contacts, enquiry form.
- Create and fully complete a map profile for your city.
- Collect the first review on the day you deliver the first job.
- Connect analytics and track enquiries, not visits.
- Check what AI assistants say about your business and fix the facts at the source.
- Set a reminder one month before your current permit expires.
