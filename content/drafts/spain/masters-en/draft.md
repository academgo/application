You can start masters in Spain with a bachelor's degree from outside Europe, and for an official master's you do not need to get that degree recognized by the ministry first. The university checks your diploma itself. At public universities, non-EU students paid about 82–84 € per ECTS credit for a standard master's in 2026/27, which is roughly 4,900–5,100 € for a one-year, 60-credit program.

The rest depends on three choices: an official or a university-own degree, a public or a private university, and the region. Each changes your fees, your language of study and your options after graduation. Below is what matters for applicants from India, Pakistan, Nigeria, Bangladesh, Egypt and the Gulf.

<!-- block: textContent -->
## Official and university-own master's degrees

Spanish universities offer two kinds of master's programs. A **máster universitario** is an official degree listed in the national register of university programs. A **título propio** is a university-own degree: the university designs and awards it without national accreditation.

For immigration, both count. Spanish law places títulos propios of recognized universities under "higher studies," the same category as official degrees. So both can lead to a student stay permit and both give the automatic right to work up to 30 hours a week.

The differences show up elsewhere:

- Admission without diploma recognition applies to official master's programs.
- Scholarships such as MAEC-AECID África-Med Máster fund official, full-time master's programs only.
- Some official master's programs give access to a regulated profession in Spain, such as law practice or health psychology. Spanish universities call them "habilitantes," and they often cost more.

If you plan to stay in Spain after graduation, choose an official master's unless you have a clear reason not to. We explain why in "What to know before you apply" below.

## How much does a masters in Spain cost at public universities?

Public universities charge per ECTS credit, and each region sets its own non-EU rules. A student permit does not count as residence for fees, so you pay the non-EU rate even after arrival.

<!-- block: tableBlock -->
| University (2026/27) | Standard master's, non-EU | Professional (habilitante) master's | 60 ECTS, approx. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Complutense University of Madrid (UCM) | 84.07 € per ECTS | Higher rate, e.g. 126 € (law practice), 169.21 € (health psychology) per ECTS | ~5,044 € |
| University of Barcelona (UB) | 82 € per credit; some programs 95–110 € | 71 € per credit | ~4,920 € |
| Universitat Politècnica de València (UPV) | Double the resident price for new non-EU students without residence | Double the resident price | Depends on program |

<!-- block: textContent -->
Two more costs are easy to miss. Some universities charge a fee to check a foreign diploma: the Autonomous University of Barcelona, for example, charges 218.15 €. Lower per-credit prices you may see online usually apply to EU students and residents only.

For program-level details, see our pages on the [Complutense University of Madrid](/study-in-spain/complutense-university-of-madrid) and the [University of Barcelona](/study-in-spain/university-of-barcelona).

## Admission requirements for non-EU graduates

For an official master's, the university checks two things: the level of your bachelor's degree and whether that degree gives access to master's study in the country that issued it. Admission on this basis does not mean your degree is recognized in Spain for other purposes, such as employment in a regulated profession.

<!-- block: bulletsBlock title="What you usually prepare" -->
- Bachelor's diploma and transcript, legalized for use in Spain.
- Apostille if your documents come from India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Saudi Arabia, Oman or Bahrain.
- Full legalization chain if they come from Nigeria, Egypt, the UAE, Qatar or Kuwait: Ministry of Education, then Ministry of Foreign Affairs, then the Spanish consulate.
- Proof of language level. Spanish law sets no fixed level; each program decides. Spanish-taught programs usually ask for B1–B2.
- CV, motivation letter and any extra documents the program lists.
- The diploma check fee, if the university charges one.

<!-- block: textContent -->
Full details on documents and translations are on our [admission requirements page](/study-in-spain/admission-requirements).

<!-- block: processBlock title="Your 2027/28 master's timeline" -->
1. Autumn 2026 — shortlist programs, check the language requirement and start the apostille or legalization of your diploma.
2. January–February 2027 — first pre-registration window at many public universities. At UCM in 2026 it ran from 28 January to 20 February; dates for 2027 are published closer to the intake.
3. Spring 2027 — second window. At UCM in 2026 it ran from 27 April to 22 May. A short September window exists only for remaining places.
4. At least 2 months before classes — apply for the student visa at the Spanish consulate. The immigration office has 7 days to decide on the permit, then the consulate issues the visa within 1 month.
5. Within 1 month of arrival — apply for your TIE residence card at the police, if you stay longer than 6 months.

## Student visa and the IPREM money rule

A master's student applies for a long-stay student permit (estancia por estudios). It is issued for the full official length of the program. You must study full-time, which means enrolling in at least 90% of the program's credits.

The key financial rule is IPREM, a Spanish reference income. You must show 100% of IPREM for every month of your stay: 600 € a month, or 7,200 € for a 12-month program in 2026. Tuition is not part of this amount. If your housing is fully paid in advance for the whole stay, this requirement does not apply. Scholarships and documented sponsorship count.

<!-- block: tableBlock -->
| Who comes | Minimum for 12 months (2026) |
|---|---|
| Student alone | 7,200 € |
| Student + 1 family member | 12,600 € |
| Student + 2 family members | 16,200 € |
| Student + 3 family members | 19,800 € |

<!-- block: bulletsBlock title="Other visa requirements" -->
- Medical (not travel) insurance from a company authorized in Spain, for the whole stay, with no co-payments, waiting periods or coverage limit. Repatriation cover is not required.
- Police clearance from every country you lived in during the last 5 years, if you are 18+ and staying more than 6 months.
- Medical certificate for the visa application.
- Passport valid for at least 1 more year.
- Government fee for the initial permit of about 10.94 €, plus the consulate's own visa fee.

<!-- block: textContent -->
Family members (spouse or partner, minor children) can join a university student, but they cannot work in Spain. The full process is on our [Spain student visa page](/study-in-spain/spain-student-visa).

## Working during and after your master's

A university student can work for an employer or be self-employed without a separate work permit, as long as work does not interfere with studies. The limit is 30 hours a week, and exceeding it can cancel your student permit. You may work only in the autonomous community that issued your permit, plus border towns of neighboring regions.

Internships that are part of your study plan need no extra permit. Social Security registration through a job can replace private health insurance.

After graduation you have two routes, and neither requires you to leave Spain for a new visa.

**Route 1: switch to a work permit.** There is no labor-market test and no need for a permanent full-time contract. You need a contract under the sector's collective agreement; part-time is fine if annual pay reaches the full-time minimum wage, 17,094 € in 2026. The new permit is issued for 1 year.

Apply within 2 months before or 3 months after your permit ends or your degree is awarded. Once the application is accepted for processing, you can work full-time while you wait.

**Route 2: the job-search permit.** Graduates with a degree at EQF level 6 or higher can get a permit to look for work or start a business, for up to 24 months, without extension. Apply online within 60 days before or 90 days after your student permit ends. The office answers within 20 days, and no answer means approval.

One exception: students funded by development-aid or humanitarian scholarships, such as MAEC-AECID, cannot switch to a work permit this way. More on both routes on our [study and work in Spain page](/study-in-spain/study-and-work-in-spain).

## What to know before you apply

These five points most often change a student's plan.

<!-- block: bulletsBlock title="Risks and how to handle them" -->
- **Applying from inside Spain has strict limits.** If you entered legally, you can apply for a student permit from Spain only for university studies, and only if you apply at least 2 months before classes start and at least 2 months before your legal stay ends. On a 90-day visa, that means the first ~30 days after entry. This route does not work for language courses: a tourist who wants a language course needs a student visa from the consulate. Our advice: plan for a consulate visa and treat the in-country route as a backup that needs exact dates.
- **Most public-university master's programs teach in Spanish.** In Catalonia, public universities are also increasing teaching in Catalan: the official target for bachelor's programs in 2025/26 is 80% of teaching hours in Catalan. We help you find English-taught options or plan a Spanish course before you start.
- **Official vs own degree matters after graduation.** Both give you a student permit and work rights. But the job-search permit requires an EQF level 6 degree or higher, and the rules do not clearly say how a título propio fits that requirement. If staying in Spain is your goal, an official master's is the safer choice.
- **IPREM is a legal minimum, not a living budget.** 600 € a month satisfies the consulate, but it does not cover rent in a big city. Budget separately for tuition and real living costs.
- **Housing in Madrid and Barcelona is expensive.** A room in a shared flat averages about 550 € a month in Madrid and about 600 € in Barcelona (August 2026). With other costs, a student needs roughly 1,100–1,450 € a month in these cities and 950–1,200 € in Valencia. Start the housing search as soon as you have an admission letter.

<!-- block: accordionBlock title="Frequently asked questions" -->
### Do I need to get my bachelor's degree recognized before a master's in Spain?
Not for an official master's. The university itself checks the level of your degree and whether it gives access to master's study in your home country.

### How much does a master's cost at a Spanish public university for non-EU students?
In 2026/27, standard programs cost non-EU students 84.07 € per ECTS at Complutense and 82 € per credit at the University of Barcelona, about 4,900–5,100 € for 60 credits. Professional master's programs often cost more, and UPV in Valencia charges non-EU students double.

### Can I study a master's in Spain in English?
Some programs are taught in English, but most public-university master's programs are in Spanish, and in Catalonia some teaching is in Catalan. Each program sets its own language requirement. Spanish-taught programs usually ask for B1–B2.

### Can I work while studying for a master's in Spain?
Yes. University students can work up to 30 hours a week without a separate permit, but only in the region that issued their permit.

### Can I stay in Spain after my master's?
Yes, in two ways. You can switch to a work permit without leaving Spain if you have a job contract under the sector's collective agreement. Or, with a degree at EQF level 6 or higher, you can apply for a job-search permit of up to 24 months.

### Can I enter Spain on a visitor visa and apply for a student permit there?
For university study, yes, if you are in Spain legally and apply at least 2 months before classes and before your legal stay ends. On a 90-day visa, that leaves about 30 days after entry. Language courses do not qualify.

### How much money do I need to show for a Spanish student visa?
The legal minimum is 600 € per month of stay, or 7,200 € for 12 months in 2026, not counting tuition. Each accompanying family member adds to the total.

### Are there scholarships for international master's students in Spain?
The MAEC-AECID África-Med Máster program offered 30 scholarships for 2026/27 to citizens of African and Middle Eastern countries, including Egypt and Nigeria. The next call has not been announced. For India, Pakistan, Bangladesh and the Gulf, the main options are university discounts and scholarships.
