<!-- block: textContent -->
Study in Spain if you want an official Spanish degree, the right to work up to 30 hours a week while you study, and a legal way to stay and look for work after graduation. Public universities can be surprisingly affordable: the University of Barcelona charges non-EU bachelor's students the same credit price as local students, about €1,200 a year in 2026/27. Private universities offer many more programs in English, at a higher price.

Spain is a harder fit if your goal is medicine taught in English. None of the three private universities we work with (UCAM, Universidad Europea and CEU San Pablo) teaches medicine fully in English. CEU San Pablo has a bilingual track, but it still requires Spanish at B2. Most public-university bachelor's programs are taught in Spanish, and in Catalonia largely in Catalan.

Non-EU bachelor's applicants also face a step many countries don't have: homologation of the school certificate by the Spanish Ministry of Education. Master's applicants skip it. Below you will find costs by level, the admission route, the student visa, and the catches we explain to every student before they pay a deposit.

## How much does it cost to study in Spain

Tuition depends on three things: public or private, the region, and the level of study. Public universities in Madrid charge non-EU students without residence a much higher rate than EU students, and a student stay authorization (estancia por estudios) does not count as residence for fee purposes. In Catalonia, each public university sets its own price for non-EU students.

<!-- block: tableBlock -->
| Level and type | Approximate tuition per year, non-EU (2026/27 price lists) | What drives the price |
|---|---|---|
| Bachelor's, public, Catalonia | about €1,200–7,000 | University of Barcelona about €1,200 with fees; UPC about €1,800; UPF about €6,800–7,000 |
| Bachelor's, public, Madrid | about €6,800–8,200 | Complutense charges by field: humanities cheapest, health sciences most expensive |
| Bachelor's in English, private (business, psychology, international relations) | about €7,300–17,600 | Murcia campuses are at the lower end, Madrid programs at the upper end |
| Medicine and dentistry, private | about €18,900–24,100 | Medicine is taught in Spanish or bilingually; dentistry in English costs the most |
| Master's, public (standard programs) | about €4,900–5,100 | Complutense and University of Barcelona charge non-EU students €82–84 per credit |
| Master's in English, private | about €11,800–12,400 | Example: data, project management and logistics programs at Universidad Europea, Madrid |

<!-- block: textContent -->
Private universities add a one-off place reservation deposit, roughly €950–3,600 in 2026/27 depending on the program, plus a registration or file-opening fee in the first year. Public master's programs that qualify you for a regulated profession (law, health psychology) cost more per credit than standard ones. In Valencia, UPV charges new non-EU master's students without residence double the standard price. Prices for 2027/28 were not yet published at the time of writing, so treat these figures as a guide.

## Types of universities in Spain

Public universities are run by the regions; private ones include several Catholic universities. The main differences are language, price and how you get in. By QS World University Rankings 2027, the University of Barcelona is first in Spain (=165) and Complutense second (199).

Madrid's six public universities share one application system (Distrito Único), where you can list up to 12 programs in order of preference. Private universities admit on a rolling basis and run their own entrance tests, but they still require homologation for non-EU school certificates.

## Admission requirements

The non-EU bachelor's route has several steps, each with its own timeline. The minimum age for a university student stay is 17. Language requirements are set by each university or program, not by law; Spanish-taught programs usually ask for B1–B2, and Complutense requires B2.

<!-- block: processBlock title="Bachelor's admission: step by step" -->
1. Shortlist programs and check the language — confirm whether the program is taught in Spanish, Catalan or English and which certificate it accepts.
2. Prepare your documents — your school certificate and grades for at least the last three years, with an apostille or consular legalization and an official translation.
3. Apply for homologation — the Ministry of Education (not UNEDasiss) converts your certificate to the Spanish título de Bachiller. The legal deadline is 3 months from a complete file, and the fee is about €48.
4. Get UNEDasiss accreditation and sit the PCE exams — needed for public universities. Without PCE your admission score is capped at 6 out of 10, and in Madrid homologation without passed PCE keeps you out of the main application phase.
5. Apply to universities — public ones through the regional application (in 2026, Madrid accepted foreign-system applicants until July 6 and Catalonia from June 2 to 26); private ones directly, with their own tests. While homologation is pending, many universities allow conditional enrollment.
6. Pay the deposit and apply for the visa — use the admission letter to apply at the consulate at least 2 months before classes start.

<!-- block: textContent -->
The UNEDasiss calendar for 2027 has not been published yet. Based on 2026, expect PCE registration in March–April 2027 and exams in late May 2027; online accreditation in 2026 ran from February 4 to December 1. Holders of the IB Diploma and European Baccalaureate do not need homologation.

For a master's degree, you do not need to homologate your bachelor's diploma. The university checks its level and that it gives access to a master's in your home country, sometimes for a fee (€218.15 at the University of Barcelona in 2026/27). Apply in the first round: for 2026/27, Complutense's first round ran from January 28 to February 20, 2026, and the University of Barcelona opened applications on January 15, 2026. More on our [master's in Spain](/study-in-spain/masters-in-spain) page and the full [admission requirements](/study-in-spain/admission-requirements) guide.

## Student visa and documents

Students from outside the EU apply for a long-stay student visa at the Spanish consulate, at least 2 months before classes start. The immigration office decides on the authorization within 7 days, and the consulate then issues the visa within 1 month. After arrival, if you stay longer than 6 months, you request your student card (TIE) from the police within 1 month. For university studies, the authorization covers the full official length of the program; you confirm your enrollment at the start of each academic year.

The core documents are:

<!-- block: bulletsBlock title="Core visa documents" -->
- Passport valid for at least 1 more year.
- Admission letter from a recognized Spanish university.
- Proof of funds: at least €600 for each month of stay (IPREM), on top of tuition.
- Health insurance from an insurer authorized in Spain, with coverage at least equal to the Spanish public health system; the Spanish embassy in Dhaka specifies no co-payments, no waiting periods and no coverage limit, while repatriation cover is not required.
- Criminal record certificate if you are 18 or older, from every country you lived in during the last 5 years.
- Medical certificate (required for the visa, not for applications made inside Spain).

<!-- block: textContent -->
Procedures differ by consulate; our [Spain student visa](/study-in-spain/spain-student-visa) guide covers them step by step.

## What to know before you apply

These points change plans most often, so we raise them at the first consultation.

### Entering as a tourist and applying from inside Spain

You can switch to a student stay from inside Spain only for university-level studies, not for language courses. You must be in Spain legally and apply at least 2 months before classes start and at least 2 months before your legal stay ends. With a 90-day Schengen visa or visa-free entry, that means applying within roughly the first 30 days after arrival, and the decision takes up to 2 months. If you plan a Spanish language course first, you need a student visa from the consulate. Treat the consulate route as the default.

### Language of teaching

Most public-university programs are taught in Spanish. In Catalonia, public universities follow an official plan to teach in Catalan; the 2025/26 target was 80% of class hours for bachelor's programs. English-taught dentistry exists, but clinical work is in Spanish: at Universidad Europea, clinical practice in years 4–5 is in Spanish, and UCAM requires Spanish B2 before the third year. Start Spanish early; the Instituto Cervantes runs seven DELE exam sessions in 2027, from February to November.

### Official degrees and títulos propios

Spanish universities award official degrees (título oficial) and their own degrees (título propio). Under the 2024 immigration regulation, both qualify for a student stay if the university is recognized, so both give you the automatic right to work while you study. Foreign add-on diplomas are different: CEU San Pablo states that the foreign partner certificates in its international dual degrees have no official value in Spain. If staying to work after graduation is part of your plan, choose an official degree and check its registration before paying.

### The money you must show

The legal minimum is 100% of IPREM for each month of stay. IPREM for 2026 is €600 a month, so a full year means €7,200. Add 75% of IPREM for the first family member and 50% for each additional one (€12,600 a year with one family member). Tuition is not included in this amount. Scholarships and documented sponsorship count, and if your accommodation is fully prepaid for the whole stay, this requirement does not apply. Higher amounts you may see online are estimates, not the legal rule.

### Housing in Madrid and Barcelona

A room in a shared apartment costs about €550 a month in Madrid and about €600 in Barcelona (August 2026), against a national average of about €425. A one-bedroom apartment outside the center is about €1,044 in Madrid and €1,143 in Barcelona. Start looking once you have an admission letter, or consider Murcia or Valencia.

University students can work up to 30 hours a week, as employees or self-employed, without a separate work permit, as long as the job does not interfere with studies. Exceeding the limit can cancel your student stay. The right applies within the autonomous community (region) that issued your authorization, plus border towns in neighboring regions. Curricular internships need no permit. If you work legally and are registered with Social Security, that registration replaces private health insurance. Students on language courses do not get automatic work rights, and family members who join a student cannot work.

After graduation, you can switch to a work residence permit without leaving Spain. There is no labor-market test, and a permanent full-time contract is not required: part-time work is allowed if annual pay reaches the minimum wage for full-time work (€17,094 in 2026). You apply between 2 months before and 3 months after your studies end, and you may work full time once the application is accepted for processing. Graduates with at least a bachelor's-level degree (EQF level 6) can instead request a job-search or business permit for up to 24 months; the decision comes within 20 days, and no answer means approval. This option is not available to language-course students or to students funded by development-aid scholarships. More on our [study and work in Spain](/study-in-spain/study-and-work-in-spain) page.

You will not be the only foreigner on campus: 12.4% of Complutense students in 2024/25 were international, and 12% at the University of Barcelona (Times Higher Education data).

### Can I study medicine in English in Spain?
Not at the private universities we work with. UCAM and Universidad Europea teach medicine in Spanish, and CEU San Pablo offers a bilingual track that requires Spanish B2. Dentistry is available in English, but clinical work is partly or fully in Spanish.

### How much money do I need to show for a Spanish student visa?
The legal minimum is €600 for each month of stay, or €7,200 for a full year (IPREM 2026). Tuition is not included. Family members add 75% of IPREM for the first person and 50% for each additional one.

### Can international students work in Spain?
Yes. University students can work up to 30 hours a week without a separate permit, within the region that issued their authorization. Language-course students do not get this right automatically.

### Do I need to homologate my school certificate?
For a bachelor's degree, yes, if your certificate is from a non-EU system without a reciprocity agreement. The Ministry of Education handles it, and the legal deadline is 3 months from a complete application. For a master's degree, homologation is not required.

### Are public universities in Spain cheaper for international students?
Often, but not always. The University of Barcelona charges non-EU bachelor's students about €1,200 a year, while Complutense in Madrid charges about €6,800–8,200 (2026/27). Private bachelor's programs in English start at about €7,300.

### Can I stay in Spain after graduation?
Yes. You can switch to a work permit without leaving Spain, or request a job-search permit for up to 24 months if your degree is at least bachelor's level. Development-aid scholarship holders cannot use these routes.

### Can I apply for a student visa from inside Spain?
Only for university studies, and only if you are in Spain legally and apply at least 2 months before both your course start and the end of your legal stay. With a 90-day stay, that means roughly the first 30 days after arrival. Language courses always require a consulate visa if you entered as a tourist.

### Are there scholarships for international students?
The MAEC-AECID África-Med Máster program offered 30 scholarships for 2026/27 to citizens of African and Middle Eastern countries, including Egypt and Nigeria. We did not find government programs for India, Pakistan, Bangladesh or the GCC in that round, so university discounts are the main option there.
