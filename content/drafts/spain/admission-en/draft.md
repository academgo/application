<!-- block: textContent -->
To enter a bachelor's program at universities in Spain with a school certificate from Pakistan, India, Nigeria, Egypt, Bangladesh or most other non-EU countries, you need two things. First, the Spanish Ministry of Education must homologate your certificate to the Spanish título de Bachiller. Second, you get an admission score through UNEDasiss, ideally boosted with PCE subject exams.

Everything else — apostille or legalization, translations, pre-registration at the university, the language test — hangs on these two steps. This page walks through them in order, with the 2027/28 intake in mind. Official 2027 dates are not published yet, so we show the 2026 calendar as a model and mark what is still to be announced.

Master's applicants follow a simpler route, covered briefly below and in detail on our [master's in Spain](/study-in-spain/masters-in-spain) page.

For the full country overview, see our [Study in Spain](/study-in-spain) guide.

## Who needs homologation and who does not

Homologation depends on where you finished school, not on your passport. Graduates of school systems in EU countries and in countries with a reciprocity agreement with Spain, as well as holders of the IB Diploma or the European Baccalaureate, are exempt: they need neither homologation nor the Spanish entrance exam (PAU).

Everyone else must homologate. That covers national school certificates from India, Pakistan, Nigeria, Egypt, Bangladesh and the Central Asian countries. A new decree for these systems has not been adopted yet, so the old rules of RD 412/2014 still apply.

Two common misunderstandings are worth clearing up:

- **UNEDasiss does not homologate anything.** Homologation is issued by the Ministerio de Educación, Formación Profesional y Deportes (and by some regional governments). For non-European applicants, it is a required step to obtain UNEDasiss accreditation.
- Age matters for the student permit: for university study, you must be at least 17.

If you are a GCC resident who studied in an IB school, check your diploma type first. It may save you the whole homologation process.

<!-- block: processBlock title="How to homologate your school certificate" -->
1. Collect your official school-leaving certificate and your grade transcripts for at least the last 3 years of school.
2. Apostille or legalize every document in the country that issued it (see the next section).
3. Get an official Spanish translation of each document.
4. Apply online through the Ministry's Sede electrónica, or at any Spanish public registry, including a Spanish consulate abroad.
5. Pay the Tasa 079 fee (form 790) — about €48. From abroad, it is paid by bank transfer to the Tesoro Público account listed on the Ministry's page.
6. Wait for the decision. By law, the Ministry must decide within 3 months of receiving a complete file; no answer by then counts as a refusal, although a decision can still arrive later.

<!-- block: textContent -->
In practice, homologation often takes longer than the legal 3 months, so start as early as you can. While your file is pending, you can usually request a volante de inscripción condicional — a receipt that lets a university enroll you conditionally. If homologation is refused, that enrollment is cancelled.

## Apostille, legalization and official translation

Which stamp you need depends on whether your country has joined the 1961 Hague Apostille Convention. For many applicants this changed recently: Pakistan joined on 9 March 2023 and Bangladesh on 30 March 2025.

<!-- block: tableBlock -->
| Country where the document was issued | What you need |
|---|---|
| India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Saudi Arabia, Oman, Bahrain, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Azerbaijan | Apostille |
| Nigeria, Egypt, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait | Legalization chain: Ministry of Education → Ministry of Foreign Affairs → Spanish consulate |

<!-- block: textContent -->
The UAE is not a party to the convention, whatever you may have read elsewhere. A document from a UAE school needs the full legalization chain.

For homologation, "official translation" does not only mean a Spanish sworn translator (traductor jurado). The Ministry accepts four options:

<!-- block: tableBlock -->
| Translation option | Where it is done |
|---|---|
| Sworn translator (traductor jurado) | In Spain |
| Spanish diplomatic or consular mission | In your country |
| Your country's consulate or embassy | In Spain |
| Official translator abroad, with the signature legalized | In your country |

<!-- block: textContent -->
Visa requirements can differ from homologation requirements, so check the list of your Spanish consulate before you pay for translations twice. Our [Spain student visa](/study-in-spain/spain-student-visa) guide covers the visa file.

## UNEDasiss and PCE: how your admission score is calculated

UNEDasiss is the service of UNED (Spain's national distance university) that converts a foreign school result into a Spanish admission score. Spanish public universities use this score to rank applicants.

The formula for homologable school systems is:

**Admission score = (0.2 × NMB + 4) + 0.1 × M1 + 0.1 × M2 + 0.1 × M3 + 0.1 × M4**

- NMB is your school average converted to the Spanish 10-point scale.
- M1–M4 are your best results in PCE (Pruebas de Competencias Específicas) — subject exams run by UNEDasiss. Each counts only if it is at least 5 out of 10 and was taken in the same calendar year.

Your school grades carry 60% of the score and PCE exams 40%. Without PCE, the maximum score is 6 out of 10. For popular programs such as medicine, that is rarely enough, so most serious applicants sit PCE in subjects linked to their chosen degree.

The acreditación is requested online. UNEDasiss processes an application within up to 3 months, usually in about 3 weeks.

## Pre-registration and places at public universities in Spain

With a UNEDasiss score in hand, you apply for a place through the pre-registration (preinscripción) process of the region or university where you want to study. Places on each program are limited, and applicants are ranked by admission score. Pre-registration windows are set by each region and university, so your list of target programs defines your personal deadlines.

In 2026, the deadline for sending documents to public universities through UNEDasiss was 7 July. Expect a similar early-summer deadline for 2027/28 and plan so that your homologation file and PCE results are ready well before it.

Fees for non-EU students also depend on the region. In Madrid, non-EU students aged over 18 without a residence permit pay a higher tariff, and a student stay permit does not count as residence. At Complutense, that meant about €6,823–8,186 a year for a bachelor's (2026/27). At the University of Barcelona, full-degree students, including non-EU, paid about €1,061 for 60 ECTS plus around €140 in fees (2026/27). See our [Complutense](/study-in-spain/complutense-university-of-madrid) and [University of Barcelona](/study-in-spain/university-of-barcelona) pages for details.

## Private universities: a different route to study in Spain for international students

Private universities set their own admission process. It may include an internal entrance test, an interview or a language check, and the timetable is usually more flexible than at public universities. Degree requirements such as homologation still matter for the official degree you will receive, so do not skip that step.

Among the private universities we work with are [CEU San Pablo](/study-in-spain/ceu-san-pablo-university), [UCAM](/study-in-spain/ucam) and [Universidad Europea](/study-in-spain/universidad-europea). Each publishes its own requirements and intake dates on the university card pages. Several private universities also run programs taught in English, which can be the only realistic option if you do not speak Spanish yet.

## Language of instruction and exams

No law sets one Spanish level for university admission: each university or program decides. Most Spanish-taught programs ask for around B1–B2, so always check the exact requirement of your program.

The DELE is the most widely accepted Spanish certificate. DELE sessions in 2027 are scheduled for:

<!-- block: tableBlock -->
| Date (2027) | Levels |
|---|---|
| 12 February | A2 only |
| 2 April | A1–C1 |
| 21–22 May | All levels |
| 9 July | A2–C1 |
| 10 September | A2 only |
| 15 October | A2–B2 |
| 12–13 November | All levels |

<!-- block: textContent -->
Registration closes about 5–8 weeks before each exam; for the May session, the deadline is 7 April 2027. If you need a B2 certificate for September 2027, the May session is the last comfortable option.

Work backwards from these dates. Homologation takes at least 3 months by law, and the student visa file must reach the consulate at least 2 months before classes start. In practice, that means starting your documents in autumn 2026 for a September 2027 start.

**Master's applicants:** with a bachelor's degree from outside the European Higher Education Area, you can enter an official master's program without homologating your degree. The university itself checks the level of your diploma and that it gives access to master's study in your country. This check is not a homologation for other purposes.

## What to know before you apply

These are the points applicants usually learn too late. Here they are upfront, with a way around each one.

<!-- block: bulletsBlock title="Risks and how to handle them" -->
- **Applying from inside Spain is a narrow option.** You can apply for a student stay from inside Spain only for a university program, if you are an adult, in Spain legally, and at least 2 months before classes start. You must also apply at least 2 months before your current legal stay ends — for a 90-day visit, that means within roughly the first 30 days. For a language course, this route is closed to anyone who entered as a tourist: you need a student visa from the consulate. We check your dates before you book a flight.
- **Most public-university programs are taught in Spanish.** In Catalonia, public universities are increasing teaching in Catalan: the target for publicly funded bachelor's programs in 2025/26 is 80% of teaching hours and 90% of subjects available in Catalan. If you do not speak Spanish or Catalan, we shortlist English-taught programs, mostly at private universities.
- **Official and university-own degrees.** Spain has official degrees (título oficial) and university-own degrees (título propio). Both qualify for a student stay if the university is officially registered. University students can work up to 30 hours a week without a separate permit, as long as the work does not interfere with studies. Rules for staying after graduation differ, so we check the degree type for your plans. More on our [study and work in Spain](/study-in-spain/study-and-work-in-spain) page.
- **You must show living funds.** The legal minimum is 100% of IPREM per month: €600 a month, or €7,200 for a full year of study, not counting tuition. Add 75% of IPREM for the first family member and 50% for each additional one. Scholarships and documented sponsorship count. If your accommodation is fully prepaid for the whole stay, this requirement does not apply.
- **Housing in Madrid and Barcelona is expensive.** A room in a shared flat averaged about €550 a month in Madrid and €600 in Barcelona (August 2026). A student in a shared flat should budget roughly €1,100–1,450 a month in these cities, and about €950–1,200 in Valencia. We help you compare cities before you choose a university.

### Do I need to homologate my school certificate to study in Spain?
Yes, if you finished school outside the EU and outside countries with a reciprocity agreement, and you do not hold an IB Diploma or European Baccalaureate. This applies to national certificates from India, Pakistan, Nigeria, Egypt and Bangladesh. Homologation is issued by the Spanish Ministry of Education, not by UNEDasiss.

### How long does homologation take?
By law, the Ministry must decide within 3 months of receiving a complete file. In practice, it often takes longer, so start at least a year before your intended start date. A conditional enrollment receipt (volante) lets you enroll while your file is pending.

### Is UNEDasiss the same as homologation?
No. UNEDasiss calculates your admission score for Spanish universities; it does not recognize your certificate. For non-European applicants, homologation is a required step to obtain UNEDasiss accreditation.

### Can I get into a Spanish university without PCE exams?
Yes, but your admission score will be capped at 6 out of 10, because school grades count for 60% of the score. That may be enough for less competitive programs. For medicine and other popular degrees, PCE is practically essential.

### Do I need an apostille or legalization for my documents?
It depends on the issuing country. India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Saudi Arabia, Oman and Bahrain use the apostille. Nigeria, Egypt, the UAE, Qatar and Kuwait need legalization through their Ministry of Education, Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the Spanish consulate.

### Does the translation have to be done by a sworn translator in Spain?
Not for homologation. The Ministry also accepts translations by a Spanish consulate abroad, by your country's consulate in Spain, or by an official translator abroad whose signature is legalized. Check your consulate's rules for the visa file separately.

### What level of Spanish do I need?
There is no single legal requirement; each university or program sets its own level. Most Spanish-taught programs ask for around B1–B2. If your Spanish is not there yet, consider English-taught programs, mostly offered by private universities.

### When should I start preparing for the 2027/28 intake?
Ideally in autumn 2026. Homologation takes at least 3 months, PCE registration is likely to run in March–April 2027, and the visa application must be filed at least 2 months before classes start.
