Universidad Complutense de Madrid (UCM) is a public university in Madrid with 64,407 students in 2025/26 and 26 faculties. Its official founding date is 1499, when a papal bull allowed Cardinal Cisneros to found a college in Alcalá de Henares; its roots go back to 1293. In the QS World University Rankings 2027 it is 199th in the world and second in Spain.

UCM suits applicants who speak Spanish at B2 level or are ready to reach it, and graduates looking for an official Spanish master's degree. It is a poor fit if you want to study entirely in English, and it is not a shortcut into medicine for international applicants.

## Universidad Complutense de Madrid tuition fees for non-EU students

Under Madrid's Decree 43/2022, non-EU students over 18 without Spanish residence pay the bachelor's rate for a fourth enrollment and the master's rate for a third enrollment. A student stay permit does not count as residence. In practice, a non-EU bachelor's student pays more than six times the EU rate per credit. Prices for 2027/28 are not published yet.

## Programs and the degree you receive

UCM awards official bachelor's (Grado), master's (Máster Universitario) and doctoral degrees. Its 107 "Máster de Formación Permanente" programs are the university's own titles, not official state degrees, so check which type you are applying for.

Taught fully in English, according to UCM's own lists:

- Bachelor's: English Studies, plus Business Administration (ADE), which "can be taken 100% in English" at the Somosaguas campus.
- Master's: English Linguistics (New Applications and International Communication), Erasmus Mundus in Molecular Nano- and Biophotonics, Erasmus Mundus 4 Cities (Urban Studies), and North American Studies.

Everything else is taught in Spanish.

After an official degree you can switch from student status to a work permit without leaving Spain, or apply for a job-search residence permit of up to 24 months. For regulated professions at home, check your national regulator's rules before you enroll.

## Admission requirements for international students

UCM has two bachelor's routes for non-EU applicants and a separate process for master's.

- **IB Diploma or EU school systems:** no homologation needed.
- **India, Pakistan, Nigeria, Egypt and most other systems:** you need homologation of your school certificate to the Spanish Bachiller by the Ministry of Education.
- **Spanish:** non-Spanish-speaking applicants from non-Spanish school systems must prove Spanish at B2.

<!-- block: processBlock title="General bachelor's route (Distrito Único Madrid)" -->
1. Homologation of your school certificate — official certificate plus grades for at least the last 3 years, with apostille or legalization and an official translation. The legal decision period is 3 months from a complete file; in practice it can take longer, so start early. A "volante" (proof of a pending application) usually allows conditional enrollment, which is cancelled if homologation is refused.
2. UNEDasiss accreditation — online applications open 4 February and close 1 December each year. Your score is 60% school grades and 40% specific exams (PCE). Without PCE, the calculated score is capped at 6 out of 10.
3. PCE exams — in 2026, registration ran 16 March–28 April and exams took place 25–29 May in Spain. The 2027 calendar is not published yet; by the 2026 model, expect registration in March–April and exams in late May 2027.
4. Pre-registration in the Distrito Único — one application covers six public Madrid universities, with up to 12 programs ranked by priority. In 2026, the window for foreign school systems was 5 June–6 July, with lists on 10 July. Dates for 2027 will be published closer to intake.
5. Enrollment and visa — once admitted, you enroll, pay and apply for your visa.

<!-- block: textContent -->
One rule catches many families: applicants with homologation but without a passed PCE cannot apply in the ordinary phase. They can only use the extraordinary phase for leftover places.

**Early Admission** is the second route. It is for international applicants whose family and residence are outside Spain, and it does not require PCE. In 2026/27 applications ran 22 January–1 April 2026, for up to 3 programs, with 462 places across 43 programs. Selection is based mainly on your final school grade, with a possible test and interview. Priority goes to native speakers of Spanish, English or Portuguese, then to graduates of UCM's International Foundation Program. Dates for 2027/28 have not been published yet.

**Master's:** with a non-Bologna bachelor's degree you do not need homologation. After admission, UCM runs a "Comprobación del Nivel de Formación". You need a legalized or apostilled diploma and transcript, a letter confirming the degree gives master's access in your country, and translations. In 2026/27, round 1 ran 28 January–20 February 2026 and round 2 ran 27 April–22 May 2026. Round 1 leaves the most time for a visa. Some programs ask for a seat deposit of around 200 €, so check your program's page. Details on the [master's in Spain](/study-in-spain/masters-in-spain) and [admission requirements](/study-in-spain/admission-requirements) pages.

<!-- block: bulletsBlock title="What to know before you apply" -->
- **Language is the main barrier.** Outside the few English-taught programs, you need Spanish B2, and some master's programs require C1. DELE exams in 2027 run on several dates, including 2 April and 21–22 May (all levels on the May dates). Starting from zero? UCM's language center, CCEE, charges 5,650 € for a 20-hour-per-week annual course on its current price list. A language course requires a student visa from the consulate and gives status for about 2 years at most.
- **Early Admission does not include medicine.** Medicine, pharmacy, dentistry, nursing and most engineering are not on the list. For these, the only way in is the general route with PCE, and places are competitive.
- **No UCM scholarships for full degrees for our markets.** UCM's scholarship page lists exchange programs and schemes for Latin America. Egyptian and Nigerian graduates can look at MAEC-AECID África-Med Máster (30 places in 2026/27). Holders of development-aid scholarships cannot switch to a Spanish work permit after graduation.
- **You submit the application yourself.** Applications go through UCM and Distrito Único portals under your own account. We assess your chances, prepare and certify documents, check the application before you send it, and build a plan B at other universities.

## Rankings and accreditation

UCM is 199th in the QS World University Rankings 2027 and second in Spain, after the University of Barcelona. In the Times Higher Education World University Rankings 2026 it is in the 501–600 band. As a public university of the Comunidad de Madrid, it issues official Spanish state degrees for its Grado, Máster Universitario and doctoral programs.

## Housing and student life

UCM runs 5 colegios mayores (residence halls): Antonio de Nebrija, Diego de Covarrubias, Santa María de Europa, Teresa de Jesús and Ximénez de Cisneros. In 2026/27 the price is 1,081.70 € per month for UCM students, VAT included, plus a 450 € deposit counted toward your fees. In 2026, applications ran 22 May–17 June.

Most students rent a room in a shared flat. A room in Madrid costs about 550 € per month on average. Day-to-day costs without rent are around 818 € per month, and a transport pass is 33 €. Budget roughly 1,100–1,450 € per month with a shared room.

University students can work up to 30 hours per week without a separate permit, within the Comunidad de Madrid. Read more on [studying and working in Spain](/study-in-spain/study-and-work-in-spain).

## Student visa

For a degree at UCM you apply for a student visa at the Spanish consulate at least 2 months before classes start. The permit decision takes 7 days, then the consulate issues the visa within 1 month. You need proof of 600 € per month of stay (7,200 € for a year), not counting tuition. You also need private health insurance with no co-payments, waiting periods or coverage caps, a criminal record certificate, a medical certificate and a passport valid for at least 1 more year.

Documents from India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Saudi Arabia, Oman and Bahrain need an apostille. Documents from Nigeria, Egypt, the UAE, Qatar and Kuwait need consular legalization. Full steps are on our [Spain student visa](/study-in-spain/spain-student-visa) page.

<!-- block: accordionBlock title="Frequently asked questions" -->
### Can I study at UCM in English?
Only in a few: English Studies and Business Administration (ADE) at bachelor's level, plus 4 master's programs. Everything else is in Spanish and requires B2.

### How much does UCM cost for international students?
In 2026/27, non-EU bachelor's students pay 113.71–136.44 € per ECTS, or about 6,823–8,186 € for 60 credits a year. Most official master's programs cost 84.07 € per ECTS, about 5,044 € for 60 credits. Prices for 2027/28 are not published yet.

### Can I apply to UCM without the PCE exams?
Yes, through Early Admission, which is open to applicants whose family and residence are outside Spain. In 2026/27 it offered 462 places in 43 programs. On the general route, homologation without passed PCE only gives access to leftover places.

### Can I study medicine at UCM as an international student?
Not through Early Admission. The only route is homologation, UNEDasiss with PCE and Distrito Único pre-registration, and places are competitive.

### How do I apply for a UCM master's with a degree from India or Pakistan?
You do not need homologation. You apply in one of the pre-registration rounds, and after admission UCM checks your degree level. Prepare an apostilled diploma and transcript, a letter confirming master's access in your country, and translations.

### Does UCM offer scholarships to international students?
UCM does not list scholarships for full degrees for students from South Asia, Africa or the Gulf. Egyptian and Nigerian graduates can apply for the MAEC-AECID África-Med Máster scholarship after securing a master's place.

### Can I work while studying at UCM?
Yes. University students can work up to 30 hours per week without a separate work permit, within the Comunidad de Madrid. Exceeding the limit can cancel your student status.
