<!-- block: textContent -->
MBBS in Georgia fees for international students are about $5,900–8,000 a year in tuition at the five universities we checked. The Georgian medical degree is a 6-year MD, so tuition alone comes to roughly $35,400–48,000 for the full program at current prices.

Tuition is only part of the bill. Every student also pays for housing and daily life, a D3 study visa (for applicants from India, Pakistan, Nigeria and Egypt), a residence permit and health insurance. This page puts all of these costs in one place, with the source and date for each number.

A note on dates. No university has published prices for the 2027/28 intake yet. The figures below are the current prices shown on each university's website as of September 2026, unless another date is given. Universities set fees for each new academic year, so confirm the final number before you pay.

For the full country overview, see our [Study in Georgia](/study-in-georgia) guide.

## MBBS in Georgia fees by university

The Georgian MD takes 6 years (360 ECTS), and the internship is built into the 6th year. Here is what the main universities charge international students for medicine and dentistry taught in English.

<!-- block: tableBlock -->
| University | Type | MD (English), per year | Dentistry (English), per year | 6-year MD tuition at current price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tbilisi State Medical University (TSMU) | Public | $8,000 | $7,000 | $48,000 |
| Ivane Javakhishvili Tbilisi State University (TSU) | Public | $8,000 ($4,000 per semester) | — | $48,000 |
| University of Georgia (UG) | Private | $6,500 | $6,000 | $39,000 |
| Alte University | Private | $5,950 | not published | $35,700 |
| SEU – Georgian National University | Private | $5,900 (2025 price list) | $4,900 | $35,400 |

<!-- block: textContent -->
A few details change the real price:

- **TSMU's American MD program** costs $13,500 a year, much more than its standard English-medium MD.
- **SEU's** most recent published price list is from June 2025. Treat it as an estimate for later intakes.
- **Payment rules differ.** At UG, you pay part of the fee to get the invitation for your visa, then the full first year after you arrive and before you sign the contract. From the second year, UG allows installments. TSU takes either a full year in advance or two equal payments at the start of each semester.
- **Visa refusal.** UG states that paid tuition is refunded on request if your visa is refused. Ask any university for its refund terms in writing before you pay.

For program details, see our pages on [MBBS in Georgia](/study-in-georgia/mbbs-in-georgia), [TSMU](/study-in-georgia/tbilisi-state-medical-university), [TSU](/study-in-georgia/tbilisi-state-university), [University of Georgia](/study-in-georgia/university-of-georgia), [Alte University](/study-in-georgia/alte-university) and [SEU](/study-in-georgia/seu-georgian-national-university).

## Cost of studying in Georgia outside medicine

For non-medical degrees taught in English, the cost of studying in Georgia at private universities in Tbilisi starts from about $4,000 a year. Only the University of Georgia publishes its full price list, so we use it as the reference.

| Master's: MBA, International Business Law, Computer Science, Management of IT and others | $4,000 |

Health-related programs outside the MD are cheaper. TSMU charges $3,500 a year for Pharmacy in English and $3,000 for Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation. SEU lists Nursing at $3,500 (2025 price list).

TSU publishes one flat fee for all programs, but that rate is for Georgian citizens. It does not apply to international students. For TSU's non-medical programs in English, we request the current price directly from the university.

## One-time costs: admission, visa and residence permit

These fees come on top of tuition. Most are small, but they add up, and several have faster options that cost more.

<!-- block: processBlock title="What you pay, in order" -->
1. Application fee — UG charges $170. It is waived if you provide your own Georgian translations and the NCEQE receipt. Other universities did not publish an application fee.
2. Recognition of your school certificate by NCEQE (Georgia's education quality agency) — 40 GEL for 1 month, 80 GEL for 15 working days, 120 GEL for 5 working days, 250 GEL for 1 working day.
3. Enrollment without Georgian national exams, also through NCEQE — 30 GEL for 1 month or 60 GEL for 15 working days. After this, the Ministry of Education issues the order that admits you.
4. Notarized translations into Georgian — every foreign-language document submitted to NCEQE needs one. Indian applicants also need MEA apostilles on all education documents for the visa.
5. D3 study visa — a 20 USD consular fee, plus the VFS Global service fee if you apply through VFS (as in India).
6. Student residence permit from the Public Service Development Agency — 300 GEL for a decision on the 30th calendar day, 450 GEL for the 20th, 600 GEL for the 10th. Apply no later than 40 calendar days before your legal stay ends.
7. Residence permit card — around 60 GEL for 10 working days, up to about 150 GEL for same-day issue.

<!-- block: textContent -->
Citizens of Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain and Oman can enter Georgia visa-free for one year. They skip the D3 visa and apply for the student residence permit inside the country. Applicants from India, Pakistan, Nigeria and Egypt need the D3 visa. Details are on our [Georgia student visa](/study-in-georgia/georgia-student-visa) page.

## Living costs in Tbilisi and Batumi

Most medical universities are in Tbilisi. Batumi, on the Black Sea coast, is somewhat cheaper. The numbers below come from Numbeo, a crowdsourced cost-of-living database, for September 2026.

<!-- block: tableBlock -->
| Monthly cost (Numbeo, September 2026) | Tbilisi | Batumi |
|---|---|---|
| One person's costs without rent | about 1,730 GEL | about 1,560 GEL |
| 1-bedroom apartment, city center | about 1,746 GEL | about 1,269 GEL |
| 1-bedroom apartment, outside center | about 1,163 GEL | about 901 GEL |
| Utilities | about 232 GEL | about 201 GEL |
| Internet | about 51 GEL | about 41 GEL |
| Monthly transport pass | 40 GEL | 30 GEL |

<!-- block: textContent -->
Read these as an upper estimate. Numbeo reflects the spending of an average expat. A student who shares an apartment, rents a room or lives in a dormitory spends noticeably less.

Dormitory options are limited. TSU has the Lisi dormitory with 320 places; according to TSU pages, a place costs about 150 GEL a month, but a place for the full degree is not guaranteed. The University of Georgia has no dormitory yet and offers help with temporary housing on request.

## Sample first-year budget

Here is how a first year of an English-medium MD in Tbilisi adds up. Tuition is in USD and local costs are in GEL, as each source publishes them.

<!-- block: tableBlock -->
| Item | Amount | How often |
|---|---|---|
| MD tuition (SEU to TSMU/TSU) | $5,900–8,000 | per year |
| Rent, 1-bedroom outside center (Numbeo) | about 1,163 GEL | per month |
| Other living costs without rent (Numbeo) | about 1,730 GEL | per month |
| NCEQE recognition and enrollment | 70–310 GEL | once |
| D3 visa | 20 USD + VFS service fee | once |
| Residence permit decision | 300–600 GEL | per application |
| Residence permit card | about 60–150 GEL | per card |
| Health insurance | depends on the policy | per year |

<!-- block: textContent -->
At Numbeo's expat level, rent plus living costs come to about 2,893 GEL a month, or roughly 34,700 GEL for 12 months. Sharing housing is the main way students bring this figure down.

Two costs have no fixed number. Health insurance is required: the D3 checklist asks for a policy covering the whole visa period, and UG asks for a policy within a month of signing the contract, renewed every year. We recommend a policy for your entire stay. The residence permit requires proof of funds of at least two subsistence minimums per month for the permit period. Georgia's subsistence minimum changes monthly, so we calculate the exact amount at the time you apply.

Do not count on part-time work to cover these costs. Since 1 March 2026, a foreigner needs a separate work authorization to work for a Georgian employer, and a student residence permit alone does not give the right to work. Plan your budget without income from work in Georgia.

<!-- block: bulletsBlock title="What to know before you apply" -->
- **Independent practice in Georgia requires Georgian.** After the MD, graduates get junior doctor status, which means working under the responsibility of an independent doctor, not practicing alone. Georgian law makes knowledge of Georgian mandatory for independent medical practice, and all residency programs at TSMU are taught only in Georgian. Most international graduates build their career through licensing exams at home or in a third country.
- **Patients speak Georgian.** Teaching is in English, but universities include Georgian language courses so students can talk to patients during clinical training. Budget time for the language, not only money.
- **WFME recognition has an end date.** NCEQE, Georgia's accrediting agency, is recognized by WFME until November 2028. A student starting in 2027 graduates later than that. Renewal will be decided later, and no one can guarantee it in advance. Note that WFME recognizes the agency, not individual universities.
- **Check your home-country rules first.** Indian applicants must meet the NMC Foreign Medical Graduate Licentiate Regulations 2021 (FMGL 2021) and must qualify in NEET before studying medicine abroad. In Georgia, the internship is part of the 6-year MD rather than a separate year after it. Check how your regulator treats this before you pay any tuition. Applicants from Pakistan, Nigeria and Egypt should check their own regulator's rules in the same way.
- **There is no fixed bank balance for the D3 visa.** The embassy checklists do not set an amount. You show your own funds or a sponsor package: a notarized sponsor affidavit, the sponsor's ID, a 3-month bank statement and a salary certificate, or proof of a grant or scholarship. Be wary of anyone quoting one "required amount" for the visa.

### How much does MBBS cost in Georgia per year?
Tuition for the English-medium MD is about $5,900–8,000 a year at the five universities we checked. SEU and Alte are at the lower end, TSMU and TSU charge $8,000. These are the current published prices as of September 2026. Prices for 2027/28 have not been published yet.

### What is the total cost of a 6-year MD in Georgia?
Tuition alone is about $35,400–48,000 for 6 years if prices stay the same. Add rent, daily costs, insurance and permit fees for each year. Universities set fees for each academic year, so the total can change.

### Is it cheaper to study in Batumi than in Tbilisi?
Living costs are lower in Batumi. Numbeo (September 2026) puts one person's costs without rent at about 1,560 GEL a month in Batumi and 1,730 GEL in Tbilisi. A 1-bedroom apartment outside the center costs about 901 GEL in Batumi versus 1,163 GEL in Tbilisi.

### How much money do I need to show for a Georgian student visa?
The embassy checklists for the D3 visa do not set a fixed amount. You submit proof of your own funds or a sponsor package with a 3-month bank statement. For the residence permit, the rule is at least two subsistence minimums per month for the permit period.

### Can I work part-time in Georgia to cover my costs?
Since 1 March 2026, foreigners need a separate work authorization to work for a Georgian employer. A student residence permit does not give that right by itself. Plan your budget without income from work in Georgia.

### Do universities in Georgia offer dormitories?
Some do, with limited places. TSU's Lisi dormitory has 320 places at about 150 GEL a month, without a guarantee for the full degree. The University of Georgia has no dormitory yet and helps with temporary housing on request.

### Is health insurance included in tuition?
No. At UG, insurance is not included in the fee, and you must show a policy within a month of signing the contract. The D3 visa checklist also requires insurance for the whole visa period.
