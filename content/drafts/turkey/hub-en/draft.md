If you want to study in Turkey, you will be choosing from 208 universities that together taught about 6.3 million students in 2025/26. Around 360,000 of them are international students, according to the head of YÖK, Turkey's higher education council, in April 2025. Many bachelor's programs are taught in English, and state university fees for most programs are roughly $500–4,000 a year.

Turkey suits you if you want an English-taught bachelor's on a moderate budget and have solid school grades. At many private universities your school certificate is the main admission criterion. At Istanbul Medipol University, for example, the threshold is 60% for applicants from Pakistan, India, Egypt and Nigeria (WAEC).

Turkey is a harder fit if you expect Medicine entirely in English, need a medical license at home without checking the rules first, or plan to live in Istanbul on a few hundred dollars a month. Below you will find real fee ranges, admission routes, visa steps and the risks worth knowing before you pay a deposit.

## How much does it cost to study in Turkey

Tuition depends on two things: state or private university, and the program. Fees for international students are quoted in US dollars and change from year to year. The figures below are ranges based on 2025/26 and 2026/27 price lists; fees for 2027/28 have not been published yet.

<!-- block: tableBlock -->
| Program | State universities | Private universities |
|---|---|---|
| Bachelor's in English | about $500–4,000 a year (2025/26) | about $4,000–10,000 a year; top private universities (Bilkent, Koç) $18,000–38,000 (2025/26–2026/27) |
| Medicine | up to about $10,000 a year at top state universities (2025/26) | about $30,000–60,000 a year in English (2025/26–2026/27) |
| Dentistry | depends on the university, we check it for your shortlist | Medipol: $33,000 (30% English) or $36,000 (100% English) a year (2026/27) |
| Master's | METU: $375 per semester (2025/26) | depends on the program, we check it for your shortlist |

<!-- block: textContent -->
Some state universities take payment in Turkish lira at the central bank exchange rate. METU does this, for example. So the dollar figure is a guide, not a fixed price.

A private university price list shows how wide the range is. Medipol's 2026/27 fees for international students: Medicine 100% English $40,000, Pharmacy in English $18,000, Computer Engineering $6,500, Business Administration $5,500, Industrial Engineering $4,750. Medipol takes 10% off if you pay the full year at once or before the deadline in your offer letter. Citizens of about 60 countries, including Pakistan, India, Nigeria, Egypt and Bangladesh, can get a 15% Special Country Scholarship. Gulf states are not on that list. The university does not say whether these discounts can be combined, so budget with one discount at a time.

## Universities in Turkey: state vs private

Turkey has state universities and private foundation (vakıf) universities. In 2025/26, 838,664 students studied at private ones. The main differences for an international applicant are admission, English tests and cost.

<!-- block: tableBlock -->
| | State universities | Private (foundation) universities |
|---|---|---|
| Admission | TR-YÖS, school GPA, national or international exams; each university's senate sets the criteria | The same routes, plus the university's own exam; many admit mainly on school grades |
| English proof | Many do not accept IELTS (METU, for example); TOEFL iBT or PTE Academic is safer | IELTS is often accepted; Medipol asks for IELTS Academic 6.5, TOEFL iBT 84 or PTE Academic 71 |
| Minimum scores | Each university publishes its own on its website | Usually a threshold on school grades or tests (Medipol: 60% school grades or SAT 1000+) |
| Bachelor's fees per year | about $500–4,000 for most programs | about $4,000–10,000 at most private universities |
| Who it suits | Strong exam results and a tight budget | Applicants who want a clear, grade-based application and more English-taught options |

<!-- block: textContent -->
For **medical universities in Turkey**, the choice is sharper. State Medicine costs less, but entry depends on each university's own criteria. Private Medicine is easier to plan but costs about $30,000–60,000 a year. Read our guide to [Medicine in Turkey](/study-in-turkey/medicine-in-turkey) before you shortlist.

## Admission requirements

Since 2024/25, state universities admit international students through TR-YÖS, school GPA, national exams or international exams. Private universities can also use their own entrance exam. So you choose the route that fits your results, not one fixed path.

<!-- block: processBlock title="How to apply to a Turkish university, step by step" -->
1. Shortlist 3–4 programs and check the language of instruction: 100% English or partly Turkish.
2. Choose your admission route: school grades, TR-YÖS, or an international exam such as SAT, IB or A-levels.
3. Prepare your documents: school certificate, transcript, passport and an English certificate if you have one.
4. Apply online. At Medipol, you submit one application per semester, rank programs by priority and get a decision within one week.
5. Receive a Conditional Acceptance Letter, pay the tuition deposit by the deadline and get your Official Acceptance Letter.
6. Apply for a student visa with that letter. After arrival, register with originals, Turkish translations and your Denklik (school certificate equivalence).

<!-- block: textContent -->
### Route 1: school grades (most private universities)

Your school certificate is often enough. Medipol's 2026 admission table asks for 60% for Pakistan (Certificate of Secondary Education), India (CBSE 12th, AISSC or HSC), Egypt (Thanaweya Amma) and the Gulf states. Nigerian applicants need 60% in WAEC.

### Route 2: TR-YÖS, the exam for international applicants

TR-YÖS is set by ÖSYM, Turkey's national exam center. It has 80 questions in 100 minutes: 40 on numerical reasoning and 40 on basic mathematics. Scores run from 100 to 500, four wrong answers cancel one correct answer, and results are valid for two years. You can take it in six languages, including English and Arabic.

The autumn 2026 session is on 11 October 2026; registration for it closed in August. The 2026 fee depends on where you sit the exam: 1,650 TL in Pakistan or India, 1,150 TL in Nigeria or Bangladesh, 3,700 TL in Egypt or Saudi Arabia. ÖSYM has not announced 2027 dates yet. Based on 2026, expect a spring and an autumn session.

### Route 3: SAT, IB, A-levels

Universities can approve SAT, IB, Abitur and TR-YÖS results and publish their own minimum scores. At Medipol, SAT 1000/1600 is enough for any program. A-level applicants need at least grade D in, for example, 2 A-levels plus 3 O-levels.

### English proof

Most English-taught programs need a certificate or an internal test. Many state universities, including METU, do not accept IELTS, so TOEFL iBT or PTE Academic is the safer choice for them. Private universities often accept IELTS; check each university's requirements. If you have no certificate, many universities let you sit their own exemption exam. At Medipol the pass mark is 75. Details for each route are in our [admission requirements guide](/study-in-turkey/admission-requirements).

## Student visa and residence permit

A **Turkey student visa** is required for citizens of Pakistan, India, Nigeria and Egypt with ordinary passports. An e-Visa for 30 days exists only under conditions. Pakistani and Indian citizens need a valid visa or residence permit from the Schengen area, the US, the UK or Ireland. Egyptians aged 15–45 need the same, plus a flight with certain airlines. Nigerians cannot get an e-Visa.

Officially, the e-Visa is for tourism and business. For study, Turkey's foreign ministry recommends a student visa from a Turkish consulate. Turkish law does allow you to apply for a student residence permit from inside the country. Some universities accept residence permit documents from students who entered on an e-Visa or visa-free, but confirm this with your university in advance. Citizens of Saudi Arabia and the UAE can enter visa-free for up to 90 days in any 180 days. Gulf expat families should check the rules for their own passport.

### Apostille or consular legalization

Your school certificate must be apostilled or legalized before you leave home:

- Apostille: Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Saudi Arabia, Oman, Bahrain.
- Consular legalization (Ministry of Education, then Ministry of Foreign Affairs, then the Turkish consulate): Nigeria, Egypt, the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Iraq, Jordan.

Translations into Turkish must be notarized. If the translation is done abroad, the Turkish consulate certifies it.

### Residence permit after arrival

Once enrolled, you fill in the student residence permit form on e-ikamet.goc.gov.tr, print and sign it. You submit it through your university's international office, not at the migration office yourself. You need your passport, two biometric photos, proof of address, health insurance and a student certificate. The card costs 964 TL in 2026; students pay no residence permit fee on top of that.

Your passport must be valid for 60 days longer than the permit you request. Proof of funds is not required by default, only your own statement, unless the migration office asks for documents. A decision can take up to 90 days. After approval, register your address within 20 working days. Step-by-step details are on our [student visa and residence permit page](/study-in-turkey/student-visa-and-residence-permit).

## What to know before you apply

These are the points that cause the most trouble later. Each one has a fix if you plan for it.

<!-- block: bulletsBlock title="Risks and how to handle them" -->
- **"30% English" programs.** In some programs Turkish is the main language and only 30% of courses are in English. At Medipol, Medicine and Dentistry exist in both versions, and Law is taught only 30% in English. Check the language line in the price list, not just the program name.
- **Prep year.** Without an English certificate or a passed exemption test, you start with a preparatory year. At Medipol it costs as much as a year of your program; for Medicine and Dentistry it is $17,000. At its International School of Medicine the prep year can last up to two years. Take TOEFL iBT, PTE Academic or IELTS (where accepted) before you apply.
- **Turkish in clinical years.** At Medipol's International School of Medicine, years 1–3 are fully in English, but clinical practice and clinical exams in years 4–6 are in Turkish. You need Turkish at C1 level before year 4; the university offers Turkish courses from A1 to C1. For the new 100% English track, the language of clinical years has not been published.
- **Denklik deadline.** If you are admitted before your school certificate is recognized, each university sets its own deadline. At Yıldız Technical University (YTÜ) it is 90 days from enrollment, or admission is cancelled. The YÖK fee is 1,250 TL in 2026. Apostille or legalize your certificate at home first.
- **Address rules in Istanbul.** Since 2022, some neighborhoods and 10 districts of Istanbul have been closed to new residence registrations of foreigners. Students are exempt only if their university or faculty is in the same district. Check the address in e-ikamet or with your university before you sign a lease.
- **Non-refundable deposits.** At Medipol, the advance tuition deposit is non-refundable, though it counts toward your fees. Read the refund terms in your offer letter before paying.
- **Visa refusals.** A consulate can refuse a student visa. Apply early, keep your documents consistent, and have a plan for the next intake if the timing slips.
- **Recognition at home.** Rules for recognizing a Turkish degree differ by country and by profession. For Medicine, check your national medical council's rules before you choose a university.

## Türkiye Bursları and other ways to pay less

Türkiye Bursları is the government scholarship for international students. It covers tuition, a monthly stipend, health insurance, accommodation, a one-year Turkish course and one return flight. It also places you at a university and program. The monthly stipend is 6,500 TL for bachelor's, 9,500 TL for master's and 13,000 TL for PhD students.

It is a strong **Turkey scholarship**, but a very competitive one. In 2024, 121,830 people from 170 countries applied, and around 5,000 receive it each year, about 4%. For bachelor's you must be under 21 and have at least 70% in school; for Medicine, Dentistry and Pharmacy the minimum is 90%. For master's the age limit is 30 and the minimum grade 75%.

Applications usually run from 10 January to 20 February. For 2027/28, plan for January–February 2027, with results in early August. Full details are in our [Türkiye Bursları guide](/study-in-turkey/turkiye-burslari).

Apply to a private university in parallel as a plan B. Country scholarships, SAT-based discounts and lump-sum discounts can lower the fee there, as the Medipol example above shows.

## Living and working as a student

A student in Istanbul needs roughly $700–1,300 a month. The lower end means a dorm or a shared flat; the upper end means renting alone. A student monthly transport pass costs about 653 TL since July 2026.

On health insurance, you can register for GSS, the state health insurance, within three months of enrollment. Then you do not need a separate private policy for your residence permit.

You can work while studying, but only with a work permit. Your employer applies for it online. Bachelor's students can work only after completing the first year and only part-time. Master's and PhD students have no such limits. Permits for entertainment work and domestic work are not approved for students. Türkiye Bursları scholars need approval from YTB, the scholarship office.

After graduation, you can apply for a short-term residence permit within six months.

### Is Turkey good for international students?

It can be a good fit if you want an English-taught degree at a moderate cost. There are 208 universities, and around 360,000 international students. The trade-offs are the prep year, Turkish in some programs and paperwork after arrival.

### How much does it cost to study at a Turkish university?

Tuition is about $500–4,000 a year at state universities for most programs and about $4,000–10,000 at most private ones. Private Medicine is about $30,000–60,000 a year. Living costs in Istanbul add roughly $700–1,300 a month.

### Can I get into a Turkish university without IELTS?

Yes. Many state universities, METU for example, do not accept IELTS and ask for TOEFL iBT or PTE Academic instead. Private universities often accept IELTS but also run their own English exemption exams. Without any proof of English, you start with a prep year.

### Do I need the YÖS exam?

Not always. Since 2024/25, state universities also admit on school GPA, national and international exams, and many private universities admit mainly on school grades. TR-YÖS helps if a state university you want requires it or your school grades are weak.

### Can I work while studying in Turkey?

Yes, but only with a work permit that your employer applies for. Bachelor's students can work part-time from the second year. Master's and PhD students have no such limits.

### Is a Turkish degree recognized in my country?

It depends on your country and your profession. Before you apply, check your country's recognition rules. For Medicine, check the rules of your national medical council. We check this for your shortlist during the consultation.

### When does the academic year start?

In September. At Medipol, for example, bachelor's intake is only in the fall, while master's and PhD programs also take students in spring. Each university publishes its exact dates in its academic calendar.
