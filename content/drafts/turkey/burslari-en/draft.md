Türkiye Bursları is the Turkish government's scholarship for international students. It covers tuition, a monthly stipend, a dormitory place, health insurance, a one-year Turkish course and your flights. It is run by YTB, a Turkish government agency, and you apply online through its TBBS portal. Applying is free, and it is open to citizens of any country at bachelor's, master's and PhD level.

It is also one of the most competitive scholarships in the world. In 2026 the program received about 200,000 applications, a record. It funds about 5,000 new international students a year. That means roughly 1 in 25 to 1 in 40 applicants is selected.

Below: what it pays, who can apply, how selection works and the rules once you win. Plus the part most guides skip: a realistic plan B, so a rejection in August does not cost you a year. For the wider picture, see our guide to [studying in Turkey](/study-in-turkey).

## What the Turkey government scholarship covers

The full-time scholarship is a complete package, not only a tuition waiver. YTB also chooses your university and program from the options you list.

<!-- block: bulletsBlock title="What you get as a full-time scholar" -->
- Tuition fees at the Turkish university you are placed at.
- A monthly stipend, paid into a Turkish bank account: 6,500 TL for associate and bachelor's students, 9,500 TL for master's and 13,000 TL for PhD.
- Accommodation. Bachelor's students get a paid dormitory place, usually a state KYK dormitory, for the whole program. Master's and PhD students get one in year 1, then can rent privately with a housing allowance of 6,000 TL a month in Istanbul and Ankara, or 5,000 TL elsewhere.
- State health insurance (GSS): free treatment in state and university hospitals and SGK-contracted private clinics, starting 30 days after registration.
- One year of Turkish language study before your degree.
- A flight to Turkey when you start, and one home after graduation (request it within 6 months).

<!-- block: textContent -->
The stipend is set in Turkish lira, so its value depends on the exchange rate. The scholarship runs for 1 year of Turkish plus the standard length of your program: usually 4 years for a bachelor's, 5 for dentistry, 6 for medicine, 2 for a master's and 4 for a PhD. An English preparatory year, if needed, adds up to 1 more year. Top applicants can qualify for the Merit Scholarship, which pays double the standard stipend.

## Eligibility: age, grades and who cannot apply

<!-- block: tableBlock -->
| Level | Age limit | Minimum grade |
|---|---|---|
| Associate and bachelor's | Under 21 | 70% |
| Bachelor's in medicine, dentistry, pharmacy | Under 21 | 90% |
| Master's | Under 30 | 75% |
| PhD | Under 35 | 75% |
| Research Scholarship | Under 50 | — |

<!-- block: textContent -->
Final-year students can apply if they will graduate by August. You cannot apply if you are a Turkish citizen or a former one. You also cannot apply if you are already enrolled at a Turkish university at the level you are applying for.

Türkiye Scholarships does not fund master's or PhD programs in health sciences. Medicine, dentistry and pharmacy are funded only at bachelor's level.

A standard application does not require SAT, GRE or TR-YÖS. Upload national exam results if you have them; other exams are needed only if your program asks for them. English-taught programs require an international English certificate.

The Merit Scholarship has higher bars. Bachelor's applicants need an average of at least 90% plus one of: SAT 1350 (1400 with Math 680 for engineering) or IB 33, together with TOEFL iBT 80 or IELTS 6.5; a top-10 rank in their national exam; or an international olympiad medal. Master's and PhD applicants need at least 90%, a GRE or ALES score and TOEFL iBT 90 or IELTS 7.0.

## How to apply for Türkiye Bursları: steps and timeline

The main call opens once a year, from 10 January to 20 February. As of September 2026, the 2027 call has not been announced. In recent years, YTB published the call in late December. Check the dates on turkiyeburslari.gov.tr before you apply.

<!-- block: processBlock title="Application steps, based on recent cycles" -->
1. Prepare documents (October to December): passport or ID, a photo taken within the last year, diploma or provisional certificate, transcript, and any test or language certificates your programs require. PhD applicants add a research proposal and a writing sample.
2. Apply online in TBBS (10 January to 20 February). Write your letter of intent and rank up to 12 university and program choices from the list the system offers you. Citizens of Islamic Development Bank member countries can also tick the Joint Scholarship Programs box.
3. Preliminary check. YTB checks your grade, age and whether your file is complete. Incomplete files stop here.
4. Expert evaluation (March to April). A committee reviews your record, goals, letter of intent, activities and how well your choices fit together, then shortlists applicants for interview.
5. Test and interview (April to June). Interviews are face to face, in more than 100 countries, and usually last 15 to 30 minutes. Bachelor's applicants first take a 30-question test in math, geometry and logic, 60 minutes, on the same day. The format differs in some countries. Bring originals of all your documents.
6. Results (early August). A selection committee reviews the interview results and approves the final list.
7. Placement and arrival (August to September). You sign the scholarship agreement, get your student visa and arrive in Turkey in September.

<!-- block: textContent -->
Citizens of Pakistan, India, Nigeria and Egypt need a visa for Turkey, and for study a student visa from a Turkish consulate is recommended. After arrival, you apply for a residence permit through your university. See our [student visa and residence permit](/study-in-turkey/student-visa-and-residence-permit) guide.

## Your real chances

<!-- block: tableBlock -->
| Year | Applications | Countries |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 | 121,830 | 170 |
| 2025 | 125,668 | 171 |
| 2026 | about 200,000 | — |

<!-- block: textContent -->
YTB says it funds about 5,000 new international students a year, and around 15,000 scholars are studying now. By our estimate, that puts the success rate at about 2–4%, or 1 in 25–40 applicants. YTB publishes no official acceptance rate and no award numbers by country, so be wary of anyone quoting country quotas.

What the committee officially looks at:
- your grades and previous qualifications;
- how well your 12 choices fit your background and goals;
- your letter of intent and career plans;
- your social and extracurricular activities;
- the test (for bachelor's) and the interview.

There is no official "winning profile." The SAT and IB scores quoted online are Merit Scholarship thresholds, not general selection criteria. What you control is a complete file, a coherent list of choices and a letter of intent that sounds like you.

<!-- block: bulletsBlock title="What to know in advance" -->
- You do not pick one university. YTB places you, based on the up to 12 options you rank.
- The Turkish year is mandatory even if your degree is taught in English. You must reach C1 by the end of it.
- Graduate programs in health sciences are not funded at all.
- The stipend is in lira, and the residence permit card fee (964 TL in 2026) is not reimbursed when you renew.
- Results come in early August, only weeks before the September start, so a plan B should run in parallel.

## Scholar obligations: what can cut or cancel your scholarship

Winning is not the end of the selection. These rules come from YTB's scholarship regulations. Read them before you sign.

Turkish comes first. Unless you hold a C1 certificate, you study Turkish for a year at a TÖMER center: 960 hours plus 140 hours of academic Turkish. No C1 after that year means your stipend is frozen until you get it. No C1 by the end of year 2 means cancellation. Missing more than 20 hours a month cuts the stipend by 25% for 3 months, and more than 100 hours of unexcused absence cancels it.

Grades are checked every year. For bachelor's students, a CGPA below 2.00 (out of 4.00) or repeating a year cuts the stipend by 50% from the next year. If it happens again, the scholarship is cancelled, unless you can still graduate in your final year. Full payment resumes once results recover. Master's and PhD scholars face their own deadlines for credits and qualifying exams.

<!-- block: bulletsBlock title="Other rules to know" -->
- More than 2 months of unexcused absence in a semester cancels the scholarship.
- You must study where you are placed. Transfers need YTB approval.
- You cannot hold another scholarship from an international organization or another Turkish public body.
- Only master's and PhD scholars have a route to work, with YTB's approval and a work permit.
- A late graduation can be funded for up to 1 extra year (2 in medicine, dentistry and pharmacy) at half the stipend.
- A cancelled scholarship cannot be awarded again.

## Plan B if you are not selected for the Turkey scholarship

The biggest trap is the plan "I will enroll privately now and apply for the scholarship later." Once you are enrolled at a Turkish university at a given level, you can no longer apply for Türkiye Bursları at that level. The fix is to apply in parallel. The scholarship runs January to February, while private universities usually accept applications until summer or autumn. If you win the scholarship, you withdraw from the paid place before enrolling.

Reapplying next year is possible, but for a bachelor's you must still be under 21.

| State universities in general (example: Istanbul University) | Wide choice of programs | Roughly $500–4,000 a year for most programs; medicine and law at top universities up to about $10,000 (2025/26) |
| Success Scholarship | Monthly stipend for high achievers already studying in Turkey at their own expense | 3,500 TL (bachelor's), 5,000 TL (master's), 6,500 TL (PhD) a month for 1 year; applications in October–November |

At [Istanbul Medipol University](/study-in-turkey/istanbul-medipol-university), SAT 1400+ gives 10% off English-taught medicine, dentistry and pharmacy; for other programs, SAT discounts range from 5% (1100–1240) to 15% (1410–1600). <!-- block: accordionBlock title="Frequently asked questions" -->
### When does the Türkiye Bursları 2027 application open?
The main call runs every year from 10 January to 20 February. The official announcement for 2027 is expected in late December 2026. Confirm the dates on turkiyeburslari.gov.tr before you apply.

### What GPA do I need?
The minimum is 70% for bachelor's and associate programs and 75% for master's and PhD. Medicine, dentistry and pharmacy require 90%. Meeting the minimum only gets you past the first check. Selection is competitive above it.

### What is the age limit?
You must be under 21 for a bachelor's, under 30 for a master's and under 35 for a PhD. Research Scholarship applicants must be under 50. Check the exact cut-off date in the current call.

### How much is the monthly stipend?
The standard stipend is 6,500 TL for bachelor's, 9,500 TL for master's and 13,000 TL for PhD students. Merit Scholarship holders receive double. Tuition, housing, insurance and flights are covered on top of the stipend.

### Can I choose my university?
You rank up to 12 universities and programs from the options the system offers you. YTB makes the final placement. After that, you must study where you are placed, and transfers need YTB approval.

### Do I need to learn Turkish if my program is in English?
Yes. All scholars without a C1 certificate study Turkish for one year first. Without C1 by the end of year 2, the scholarship is cancelled.

### What if I'm not selected?
You can reapply the next year if you still meet the age limit. Better still, apply to private or state universities at the same time. Do not enroll anywhere at that level before the results, or you will lose your eligibility.
