The cost of studying in Malaysia for an international bachelor's student starts with tuition of roughly RM 35,000–45,000 a year at a private Malaysian university, plus 6% SST. On top of that, budget about RM 2,000–3,500 a month to live in Kuala Lumpur and around RM 2,700–3,300 in visa, Student Pass and insurance fees in the first year.

Full scholarships exist mostly at master's and PhD level. At bachelor's level, expect merit discounts rather than free study.

Fees below are in ringgit (RM) from 2026 price lists unless another year is shown. Prices for 2027 are not published yet and may rise. Universities quote USD at an indicative RM 3.80 per dollar, while the market rate was about RM 4.08 on 18 September 2026, so plan in ringgit.

For the full country overview, see our [Study in Malaysia](/study-in-malaysia) guide.

## Tuition: the biggest part of the cost of studying in Malaysia

The type of university sets your tuition more than anything else. Here are approximate ranges for international students, without SST.

<!-- block: tableBlock -->
| University type | Approximate tuition (international, 2026) | 6% SST |
|---|---|---|
| Foundation year (private) | RM 28,000–37,500 for the whole program | Added on top |
| Private Malaysian university, bachelor's | RM 35,000–45,000 per year | Added on top |
| UK / Australian branch campus, bachelor's | RM 48,000–65,000 per year (medicine up to about RM 139,000) | Added on top |
| Public university (UM, UTM, UPM), bachelor's | About RM 16,000–33,000 per year | UM lists fees "not inclusive of 6% SST"; check each university |

<!-- block: textContent -->
Since 1 July 2025, private universities charge 6% Service Tax on fees paid by non-Malaysians, and fee schedules usually show prices without it.

A Foundation year adds a full year of costs. APU's entry table sends Indian Class XII, Pakistani HSSC/FSc, Bangladeshi HSC, Nigerian WAEC/NECO, Egyptian Thanaweya Amma and GCC certificates straight to the bachelor's degree.

## Mandatory costs: EMGS, Student Pass and insurance

Every international student applies for a Student Pass through EMGS, and the fees are fixed in the official EMGS Schedule of Fees (v1.6, November 2025). These amounts apply to universities in Peninsular Malaysia.

<!-- block: tableBlock -->
| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| EMGS processing fee, new student (pass of 6–12 months) | RM 1,600 + 8% service tax |
| eVAL (visa approval letter) | RM 150 |
| i-Kad (student ID card) | RM 50 |
| Medical screening after arrival | RM 250 |
| Student Pass sticker | RM 60 |
| Single-entry visa | RM 50 for India, RM 20 for Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nigeria, Egypt and most other countries |
| Medical insurance (EMGS plans, per year) | RM 456–958 |
| First-year total | About RM 2,700–3,300 |
| Yearly renewal (processing, insurance, sticker, visa) | About RM 800–1,300 |

<!-- block: textContent -->
Universities usually collect these fees as a package with their own processing charges. Taylor's charges RM 2,920 for a 3-year bachelor's plus RM 644 insurance a year. APU charges around RM 3,600 at offer acceptance and RM 2,000 after visa approval. UM's non-refundable package is RM 5,451 for a one-year pass.

Also budget for a personal bond of RM 1,000–2,000, usually refunded after graduation, and one-off international fees: RM 5,000 at APU, or RM 2,800 plus RM 3,000 at Taylor's. The full visa sequence is on our [Malaysia student visa](/study-in-malaysia/malaysia-student-visa) page.

## Living costs in Kuala Lumpur and Johor Bahru

A student in Kuala Lumpur needs roughly RM 2,000–3,500 a month: less with a shared hostel room, more with a private studio and frequent Grab rides. Numbeo (September 2026) puts a cheap lunch at about RM 20, a transit pass at RM 50 and a one-bedroom flat outside the center at about RM 1,500.

## Scholarships in Malaysia for international students

The main government award is the Malaysia International Scholarship (MIS). It is for full-time master's and PhD students only. It covers tuition for the whole program and pays RM 1,500 a month.

- **Who can apply:** citizens of Commonwealth countries (including India, Pakistan, Nigeria and Bangladesh), Egypt, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain and several other countries.
- **Requirements:** under 40 for a master's or under 45 for a PhD; CGPA of 3.00 or a 2nd Class Upper; IELTS 6.0 or TOEFL PBT 550; two references; a research proposal of at least 1,000 words for research degrees.
- **Where and when:** 20 public universities, including UM and UTM, and several private ones. The 2026/27 round is closed; 2027 dates are not announced.

At bachelor's level, awards come from universities and mostly reduce tuition.

<!-- block: tableBlock -->
| University | Who can get it | What it gives |
|---|---|---|
| APU (bachelor's, Foundation, Diploma) | All nationalities with A-Level, O-Level/IGCSE or IB results | 10%, 20% or 30% off tuition; renewed each year with CGPA 2.50+ |
| APU (master's) | All nationalities, by bachelor's CGPA (3.2+ / 3.5+ / 3.7+) | 10% / 20% / 30% off tuition |
| UCSI University | Top results: A-Level AAA, IB 36+, IGCSE 6 A*/A or equivalent; interview | Partial tuition waiver for the whole bachelor's |
| Taylor's University | Malaysians and students progressing within Taylor's Education Group | Other international students should ask the university's counsellors |
| Universiti Malaya | Bachelor's: one PTE x UM award of GBP 3,000 in 2026, by essay competition. PhD: UM Bright Scholarship | Otherwise no undergraduate scholarships for international students |
| UTM | Master's: MTCP (developing countries, age up to 45, CGPA 3.00, IELTS 6.0). PhD: ASCEND Bright Talent | No financial aid for international bachelor's students |

<!-- block: textContent -->
APU's and UCSI's merit criteria list A-Level, IGCSE and IB, not CBSE, FSc or WAEC. Awards cover tuition only, not visa fees or insurance.

## What to know before you apply

- **No apostille.** Malaysia is not in the Apostille Convention, so an apostille does not help. Universities ask for certified true copies and a certified English translation. We check each university's rules before you pay for anything.
- **Everyone needs a Student Pass.** Tourist visa-free entry does not allow study. Every student applies through EMGS, and the pass is usually issued for up to one year, so budget for yearly renewal.
- **International fees differ from local ones.** Program pages often show the Malaysian price first, and private universities add 6% SST. We compare offers by the international all-in figure.
- **Work is limited to breaks.** Up to 20 hours a week, only during breaks or holidays longer than 7 days, with immigration approval. Confirm exact conditions with your university, and do not count this income in your budget.
- **The Graduate Pass is not open to everyone.** This 12-month pass (RM 600) is open to citizens of 32 countries: India and the GCC states qualify; Pakistan, Nigeria, Egypt and Bangladesh do not.
- **A 3+0 UK degree does not give the UK Graduate Route.** That visa requires study in the UK on a Student visa, generally for at least 12 months. If UK work matters, we plan a transfer route with you.

### How much does a bachelor's degree in Malaysia cost per year for international students?
At a private Malaysian university, tuition is roughly RM 35,000–45,000 a year plus 6% SST (2026 prices). Branch campuses of UK and Australian universities cost roughly RM 48,000–65,000 a year. Public universities charge about RM 16,000–33,000 a year.

### Is it cheaper to study at a public university in Malaysia?
Usually yes. UTM lists RM 71,000 for a whole Computing program, while private universities often charge more than RM 100,000 for three years. The trade-off is published minimum grades (for example, 75% in HSSC/FSc subjects for UTM engineering) and almost no scholarships for international bachelor's students.

### How much are EMGS and visa fees?
By the official EMGS schedule, the first year costs about RM 2,700–3,300, including insurance. Each renewal costs about RM 800–1,300. Universities usually charge these as a package with their own processing fee, so the amount on your invoice may be higher.

### How much money do I need per month in Kuala Lumpur?
Plan for roughly RM 2,000–3,500 a month, including housing. A shared hostel room keeps you near the lower end. In Johor Bahru, UTM estimates RM 1,000–1,500 a month for students living on campus.

### Are there full scholarships in Malaysia for international students?
Yes, but mostly for postgraduates. The Malaysia International Scholarship covers full tuition and pays RM 1,500 a month for master's and PhD students from eligible countries. Bachelor's students usually get partial tuition discounts, such as 10–30% at APU for A-Level, IGCSE or IB results.

### Do I need to show a bank statement for the Student Pass?
A bank statement is not on the standard EMGS document list for students. Immigration asks for one only when you bring dependants. Your university may still request proof of funds, so prepare one anyway.

### Can I work part-time to cover my living costs?
Only partly. Students may work up to 20 hours a week during semester breaks or holidays longer than 7 days, with immigration approval. Build your budget without counting on this income.
