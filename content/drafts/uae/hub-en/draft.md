To study in UAE as an international student usually means studying in Dubai, at a branch campus of a UK or Australian university. In 2024/25, 41 private universities supervised by KHDA in Dubai taught 42,026 students. Thirty-seven of them were branch campuses of foreign universities, and 35% of all students came from abroad.

The UAE suits you if you want a UK or Australian degree, taught in English, without moving to the UK or Australia. It also works for families already living in the Gulf and for students who plan a career in the region. The university sponsors your student visa, and most campuses take new students in September and January.

It is not a budget destination. Bachelor's tuition at most branch campuses is about AED 60,000–80,000 a year, and a year of living costs in Dubai can cost as much again. There is no British-style post-study work visa. School certificates from Pakistan and Nigeria often lead to a foundation year instead of direct entry. If a European option is also on your list, compare this route with [studying in Poland](/study-in-poland).

## How much does it cost to study in UAE?

Bachelor's programs at UK and Australian branch campuses cost roughly AED 60,000–80,000 a year (about $16,000–22,000) for 2026/27 intakes; engineering and computing sit at the top of that range or slightly above. Most 2027/28 fees are not yet published, so check the university's price list before you apply.

Several universities fix your tuition for the whole course: what you pay in year one stays the same until you graduate (Heriot-Watt, Birmingham, Curtin; at Middlesex the net fee stays fixed and only VAT changes can apply). Budget for one-off fees too. Heriot-Watt charges a non-refundable AED 300 application fee, Curtin charges AED 367 to apply and AED 3,500 at registration, and Birmingham charges no application fee but takes an AED 5,000 deposit that counts toward your tuition.

For a full breakdown by university and program, see the [cost of studying in Dubai](/study-in-uae/cost-of-studying-in-dubai).

<!-- block: bulletsBlock title="Scholarships: what international students can actually get" -->
- Scholarships at private campuses in Dubai are tuition discounts, not cash paid to you.
- Heriot-Watt Merit Scholarship: 30%, 40% or 50% of first-year net tuition, spread over all years. The thresholds are 80%, 85% and 90% for both CBSE and Pakistan's FBISE HSSC.
- UOWD Academic Merit Scholarship: 15%, 25% or 50% on the four-year route, open to any nationality, with limited places.
- Birmingham Chancellor's Scholarship: 15–30% every year of study, 30% for CBSE above 90%. It was published for September 2026 entry only, so 2027 terms may differ.
- Middlesex offers international applicants an International Study Grant of variable size; its 10–50% Academic Excellence Scholarship is for UAE applicants only.
- Curtin lists several merit scholarships without published percentages, and foundation grants of up to AED 10,000. Different awards cannot be combined.
- Khalifa University in Abu Dhabi has five tiers for international bachelor's students, from 100% of tuition down to textbooks only.

## Types of universities in UAE

Most international students choose a private branch campus in one of Dubai's education free zones. Federal universities accept international students too, but charge full fees per credit hour. In 2025/26, campuses of IIM Ahmedabad and the American University of Beirut also opened in Dubai, according to regional media.

## Admission requirements

Your school certificate decides whether you enter the first year directly or start with a foundation year. Most bachelor's programs ask for IELTS Academic 6.0, and foundation programs usually ask for 5.5. Apply well before your intake: after the offer, the visa alone needs about 8 weeks of lead time.

<!-- block: processBlock title="How admission works, step by step" -->
1. Check your entry route — compare your certificate with the program's requirements: year one or a foundation year.
2. Prepare your English score — IELTS 6.0 is the usual bachelor's level; some campuses also accept TOEFL, PTE or Duolingo, and several accept a strong English grade in CBSE or WAEC.
3. Apply online — upload transcripts or predicted grades, your passport, photos and your English result. Middlesex usually issues an offer within two working days, Birmingham within 10 working days.
4. Accept the offer — pay the deposit or first installment: for example, 10% of first-year tuition plus a refundable AED 1,000 at Heriot-Watt, or AED 3,500 registration at Curtin.
5. Pay tuition for the visa — universities ask for 25% of the annual fee (Middlesex, Heriot-Watt) or the full first study period (Curtin, UOWD) before they apply for your entry permit.
6. Arrive and finish the paperwork — medical test, Emirates ID, then legalization and equivalency of your school certificate during your first semester.

<!-- block: bulletsBlock title="Entry routes by country (bachelor's)" -->
- India (CBSE, ISC): direct entry often starts at 65% (Middlesex, Heriot-Watt management) or 60% in the best four subjects (Curtin). Birmingham expects roughly 75–90% depending on the program; with 65% you can enter its Integrated Foundation Year.
- Pakistan (HSSC): Heriot-Watt accepts 70% for management and Curtin asks for 75%. UOWD asks for 88% on its three-year route and 65% on its four-year route. At Middlesex, FSc/HSSC holders start with the International Foundation Programme (60%).
- Nigeria (WAEC, NECO): Heriot-Watt takes five subjects at B2 for management, psychology and design; engineering and computing start with a foundation year. Middlesex admits WAEC holders to its foundation program only, and UOWD to its four-year route only.
- Egypt (Thanaweya Amma): UOWD accepts 70% on its four-year route. Several other campuses do not publish requirements for Egypt, so we confirm them with admissions before you apply.
- UAE and GCC school leavers: Heriot-Watt asks for 80% in Tawjihiyya for year one. Graduates of private schools in the UAE also need a Ministry of Education equivalency certificate.

## Student visa and documents

Your university sponsors your visa. It applies for an entry permit; after arrival you take a medical test (required over 18) and get an Emirates ID. The student visa is issued for one year and renewed annually. Expat parents living in the UAE can sponsor sons under 25 and unmarried daughters of any age instead.

A university visa package covers the visa, Emirates ID, health insurance and a refundable deposit of AED 2,500–3,000. At our five partner campuses, the total from abroad is about AED 6,300–8,800; changing status inside the UAE costs more. Curtin advises submitting visa documents at least 8 weeks before the semester. At UOWD, the entry permit takes 10–15 working days and processing another 7–14, sometimes a month or longer.

Citizens of Pakistan, Nigeria and Egypt generally need a visa to enter, and Indian citizens get visa on arrival only with a US visa, green card or certain residence permits. The university's entry permit is the most reliable route for everyone.

The UAE does not accept the apostille, even from Hague Convention members such as India and Pakistan. Legalization has four steps: the school or education authority plus a notary, your foreign ministry, the UAE embassy, then the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Your school certificate also needs an equivalency from the UAE Ministry of Education (a degree, from MoHESR). You can start classes meanwhile, but Birmingham and Heriot-Watt expect it done within the first semester.

Costs by university and timelines are on the [UAE student visa](/study-in-uae/uae-student-visa) page.

## What to know before you apply

### KHDA licensing and federal accreditation are not the same thing
Dubai's free-zone universities are licensed by KHDA. Federal recognition comes from the Ministry of Higher Education and Scientific Research (MoHESR) and its Commission for Academic Accreditation (CAA), first for the institution and then for each program. If you need your degree for a UAE government job, a regulated profession or further study at a CAA-accredited university, check that **both the university and your specific program** are accredited. In February 2025, MoHESR licensed 16 Dubai universities, so most large branch campuses now hold both; all five universities we work with are on the federal CAA list.

### Recognition back home is a separate question
Branch campuses award the parent university's degree. How your home country treats it depends on its own rules, especially for regulated professions, so check with the relevant authority before you commit.

### Plan for the total cost, not just tuition
Living costs of AED 5,500–9,000 a month can match or exceed your tuition over a year. Add the visa package, the medical test (about AED 250–550) and your first rent deposit. Build this budget before you accept an offer.

### Do not count on part-time work to pay the fees
Students can work only under specific permits, and Curtin's fee policy states that a student on its visa cannot work outside them. Treat work as experience, not as income for tuition.

### Safety: check official advice before you travel
The situation in the region changed several times in 2026, and UAE universities briefly moved to remote learning in spring, according to local media. As of September 21, 2026, the UK Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (update of July 24, 2026) does not advise against travel to the UAE, but warns of possible escalation, flight cancellations and airspace closures. Check your own government's travel advice before you book.

## Living costs, work and student life in Dubai

Without rent, one person in Dubai needs about AED 4,207 (about $1,145) a month, according to Numbeo in September 2026. If you study in Dubai on your own, a realistic budget is about AED 5,500–9,000 a month, including a shared room in a student residence. None of our partner universities runs its own dormitories; they work with private residences, and at Middlesex partners rent includes furniture, Wi-Fi and utilities.

<!-- block: bulletsBlock title="Monthly costs to plan for" -->
- Student residence: from about AED 2,200 (triple room) to AED 7,450 (studio) a month, plus a deposit of AED 3,000–3,500 (Middlesex partner prices, September 2026).
- Private one-bedroom apartment: about AED 5,507 outside the center and AED 8,800 in the center (Numbeo).
- Transport: a regular monthly pass costs about AED 275. The student Nol card gives 50% off the metro, tram, buses and water transport, but only up to age 23; it costs AED 70.

<!-- block: textContent -->
### Working while you study
The Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE) issues a three-month Student Training and Employment Permit for students aged 15 and over who are already in the UAE. The employer applies, usually with a no-objection letter from your university. A separate part-time work permit also exists. Ask the university's careers office before you accept any job.

### After graduation
There is no separate post-study work visa. Graduates move to an employer's work visa, a job exploration visa (60, 90 or 120 days, for graduates of the world's top 500 universities, among other conditions) or a Golden Visa. The 10-year Golden Visa for UAE graduates requires a CGPA of at least 3.5 (class A university) or 3.8 (class B), graduation within the last two years and a degree attested by MoHESR.

### Is a degree from a Dubai branch campus the same as one from the home campus?
Yes, the degree is awarded by the parent university. At Birmingham, for example, the transcript mentions Dubai, but the degree certificate mentions only the UK. Heriot-Watt and Middlesex also award the same degree under the same academic regulations as their UK campuses.

### How much does it cost to study in Dubai per year?
Bachelor's tuition at most UK and Australian branch campuses is about AED 60,000–80,000 a year for 2026/27 intakes, and about AED 129,000–157,000 at Birmingham. Living costs add roughly AED 5,500–9,000 a month. Fees for 2027/28 are mostly not yet published.

### Can I enter the first year with a Pakistani or Nigerian school certificate?
It depends on the university and program. Heriot-Watt accepts 70% in HSSC and five B2 grades in WAEC for management; UOWD accepts both on its four-year route. At Middlesex and for Birmingham's published routes, these certificates lead to a foundation year.

### Do I need IELTS?
Most bachelor's programs ask for IELTS Academic 6.0, and foundation programs for 5.5. Some campuses accept school English grades instead, for example 65% in CBSE English or C6 in WAEC English at Middlesex. Heriot-Watt also accepts Duolingo and IELTS Online; UOWD does not accept IELTS Online.

### Can I transfer to the UK or Australia later?
Several campuses allow it. Middlesex students can transfer to London in their second year, Heriot-Watt students can move to the UK or Malaysia after year one, and UOWD and Curtin students can continue at Australian campuses. You need a visa for the destination country, and Dubai scholarships usually do not transfer.

### Can I stay in the UAE after graduation?
There is no post-study work visa, but you have 60 days after your student visa is cancelled. You can move to an employer's work visa, apply for a job exploration visa or, with a CGPA of 3.5 or higher from a class A university, apply for the 10-year Golden Visa.

### Is it safe to study in the UAE now?
As of September 21, 2026, the UK government does not advise against travel to the UAE but warns of regional risks and possible flight disruption. Check your own government's latest advice before you book.
