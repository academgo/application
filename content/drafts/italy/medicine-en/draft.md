Non-EU students can study medicine in Italy in English at public universities, but only through a competitive entrance test and a small, separate quota of seats. For the 2026/27 intake, that test is the IMAT, held on 29 September 2026; the format for 2027/28 has not been officially announced yet.

Below: how admission works today, how many seats non-EU applicants have, what the Italian-language reform changed and what the degree costs. Every figure is given with the academic year it applies to.

If you are searching for "MBBS in Italy", note one thing first. Italy does not award an MBBS. The degree is a single-cycle Medicine and Surgery degree (laurea magistrale a ciclo unico), and how your home country recognizes it is a separate question we cover below. For the wider picture, start with our [guide to studying in Italy](/study-in-italy).

<!-- block: textContent -->
## How the IMAT works for English-taught medicine

In 2026/27, English-taught medicine still uses a national entrance test. The Italian-language reform did not replace it. According to the University of Bologna, the 2026/27 test takes place on 29 September 2026 at 13:30 and lasts 100 minutes. Registration ran on Universitaly from 26 August to 9 September 2026.

The paper has 60 multiple-choice questions, each with five options. Scoring is approximately +1.5 for a correct answer, −0.4 for a wrong one and 0 for a blank, for a maximum of about 90 points. Blind guessing costs you points, so practice deciding when to skip.

<!-- block: tableBlock -->
| Section (2026/27, University of Bologna) | Questions |
|---|---|
| General knowledge and reading skills | 4 |
| Logical reasoning | 5 |
| Biology | 23 |
| Chemistry | 15 |
| Physics and mathematics | 13 |
| **Total** | **60** |

<!-- block: textContent -->
Biology and chemistry carry 38 of the 60 questions. That is where most of your preparation time should go.

For 2027/28, the ministry (MUR) has said it will publish the admission procedure for medicine in a separate note. Nobody can yet confirm whether English-taught programs will keep the same test or move to a new model. Build your study plan on the 2026/27 syllabus, and treat any date for 2027 that you see online as unofficial until the ministry publishes it.

## Seats for non-EU applicants living abroad

Each university sets a separate quota for non-EU citizens who live outside Italy. These quotas are small. At the University of Bologna, the 2026/27 English-taught Medicine and Surgery program has 20 seats for non-EU applicants from abroad and 140 for EU and equivalent applicants.

Across Italy, the 2026/27 ministerial decree reportedly sets around 1,362 seats for non-EU applicants in English-taught medicine, about 11 in dentistry and about 7 in veterinary medicine. These are approximate figures from secondary sources and change every year.

Two rules shape your strategy:

- On Universitaly you list one program for your pre-enrollment. The visa and residence permit are issued for that one course.
- As a non-EU applicant from abroad, you generally compete only for the seats of the university named in your pre-enrollment. Seats are not reassigned to other universities for you.

Choosing the university is therefore a strategic decision. Read more on our [University of Bologna page](/study-in-italy/university-of-bologna).

## Italian-taught medicine after the reform

The 2025 reform (Law no. 26 of 14 March 2025) changed access only to Italian-taught Medicine, Dentistry and Veterinary Medicine. Instead of an entrance test, students take an open first semester. In 2026/27 it is called "semestre aperto": three 6-credit courses in biology, chemistry and physics, then exams and a national ranking.

For 2026/27, the semestre aperto exams are on 10 December 2026 and 11 January 2027. Non-EU applicants from abroad still go through Universitaly pre-enrollment and need Italian at B2 level. If you do not already speak Italian well, the English-taught route is the realistic one.

## Tuition fees and living costs

At Italian public universities, fees depend on the university and on your family income (ISEE), and some universities apply a fixed fee to non-EU students whose family lives abroad.

<!-- block: tableBlock -->
| University and program | Fee rule (2026/27) | Annual fee |
|---|---|---|
| University of Bologna, Medicine and Surgery (English) | Family income ISEE up to €27,000 | €157.04 (fixed part only) |
| University of Bologna, Medicine and Surgery (English) | Maximum, or no ISEE submitted | €3,315 |
| University of Padua, medicine (fee group B) | Non-EU students with family abroad | €2,990 + regional tax up to €192 + €16 stamp |
| University of Padua, medicine (fee group B) | Citizens of countries on the ministry's list of particularly poor countries (includes Bangladesh) | €996.66 + regional tax + stamp |

<!-- block: textContent -->
Fees for 2027/28 have not been published yet. Treat the table as a reference for budgeting.

Living costs are usually the bigger part of the budget. As a rough guide, expect about €950–1,250 a month in Rome and Bologna and about €1,100–1,400 in Milan, room included. In August 2026, a single room averaged about €585 a month in Bologna, €615 in Rome and €704 in Milan.

<!-- block: processBlock title="How to apply for English-taught medicine: the 2027/28 route" -->
1. Check eligibility early — you need at least 12 years of school education. Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Nigeria and Egypt generally have 12-year systems. If your country has a compulsory national university entrance exam, the university can ask for its result.
2. Prepare your English certificate — the consulate requires at least B2 in all four skills; each university sets its own minimum IELTS or TOEFL score.
3. Choose one university — compare non-EU quotas and fees, because you can list only one program on Universitaly.
4. Submit pre-enrollment on Universitaly — the official portal is free. Each university sets its own window, so there is no single national opening date. Since 2026/27, a university can validate only up to its foreign-student quota plus 20%, after which the portal blocks new validations. Apply as early as the window allows.
5. Register for and sit the admission test — follow the dates in the ministry's note for 2027/28 once it is published.
6. Apply for the study visa — at the Italian consulate for your country of residence, with a validated application, no later than 31 October 2027 (the university may set an earlier date).
7. Arrive and apply for your residence permit — within 8 working days of entry, through the post office kit or the university's service point.

## Documents: school certificate, CIMEA or DOV, apostille

For the visa, your school certificate must come with either two CIMEA certificates (comparability and authenticity verification) or a Declaration of Value (DOV) from the Italian embassy. A DOV covers comparability only, unless it also confirms authenticity. The documents needed for enrollment itself are decided by the university.

CIMEA fees are approximately €150 for a statement of comparability and about €65 for authenticity verification, with a standard processing time of about 60 working days. If your country is a party to the Lisbon Recognition Convention, the comparability statement can be downloaded free from the ARDI database.

How your documents are legalized depends on your country:

<!-- block: bulletsBlock title="Apostille or consular legalization" -->
- Apostille: Pakistan (since 9 March 2023), India, Bangladesh (since 30 March 2025), Saudi Arabia, Oman, Bahrain.
- Consular legalization at the Italian embassy: Nigeria, Egypt, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait.
- Translations into Italian are usually required alongside the legalized documents. Check the checklist of your consulate.

## What to know before you apply

The process is slow and strict. These points catch applicants out most often.

<!-- block: bulletsBlock title="Risks and how to handle them" -->
- **Bureaucracy runs on long timelines.** Universitaly pre-enrollment, CIMEA or DOV, the consulate and then the permesso di soggiorno each take weeks. A university's validation does not guarantee a visa appointment or a visa. Start document work at least a year before the intake; we build the calendar with you. See our [Universitaly admission guide](/study-in-italy/admission-universitaly).
- **The financial minimum is higher than many agents say.** For 2026/27 and 2027/28 the consulate requires €10,179.85 per academic year, plus money for a return ticket. Funds must be legal and traceable, ideally in your own account; relatives up to the fourth degree can sponsor you. Cash and a one-off large deposit just before the visa do not count as proof.
- **A scholarship application is not money.** Being "idoneo" (eligible) for the DSU grant or having applied does not count toward the visa minimum. Only a grant already awarded does.
- **DSU grants are income-based, and you can lose them.** The regional agency awards them by family income and assets. For 2026/27 the national ceiling is ISEE €28,339.88, and regions may set lower limits (Emilia-Romagna, where Bologna is: €25,000). A first-year student must earn 20 credits (CFU) by 30 November of the following year. Otherwise the grant is revoked and everything received must be repaid. See the [DSU scholarship guide](/study-in-italy/dsu-scholarship).
- **Housing is scarce.** For the visa you need a hotel booking, a lease, a declaration of hospitality or an assigned dorm place; a dorm application is not enough. Rooms in Bologna, Rome and Milan cost €585–704 a month on average, so start searching before your visa appointment.
- **Recognition at home is your responsibility.** Whether you can practice in your home country depends on your national medical regulator, not on the Italian university. Check its current rules for foreign graduates before you apply; we help you prepare the questions to ask.
- **Check the institution type.** Medicine is taught at universities. Private academies (AFAM institutions) award different types of diplomas, so if you compare other programs in Italy, check each program's official status.

## Visa, arrival and staying on track

The study visa (type D) requires the funds above, accommodation proof, health insurance with no cap on emergency hospitalization, and fingerprints. If your entrance exam is still ahead when you apply, the consulate can issue a conditional D visa for 100 days. Full details are on our [student visa page](/study-in-italy/italy-student-visa).

After arrival, apply for the residence permit within 8 working days. The first permit for up to one year costs €116.46 in fees. Voluntary registration with the national health service (SSN) costs €700 per calendar year.

Your permit is renewed only if you progress: at least one exam in the first year and at least two in each following year. If you are not enrolled by 31 January of the academic year, the visa is canceled.

<!-- block: accordionBlock title="Frequently asked questions" -->
### Is IMAT still required for medicine in Italy?
For English-taught medicine in 2026/27, yes: the entrance test is on 29 September 2026. The reform with an open first semester applies only to Italian-taught programs. The admission format for 2027/28 has not been officially announced; the ministry will publish it in a separate note.

### Can I study MBBS in Italy?
Italy does not award an MBBS. The equivalent is the single-cycle Medicine and Surgery degree, available in English at several public universities. Before applying, check with your national medical regulator how it treats this degree.

### How much does it cost to study medicine in Italy?
At the University of Bologna, English-taught Medicine and Surgery costs from €157.04 to €3,315 a year in 2026/27, depending on family income. At the University of Padua, the fixed fee for non-EU medicine students is €2,990 plus smaller charges. Add living costs of roughly €950–1,400 a month depending on the city.

### How many seats are there for non-EU students?
Quotas are set by each university. Bologna has 20 non-EU seats in English-taught medicine for 2026/27. Nationally, there are around 1,362 non-EU seats in English-taught medicine this year, according to secondary sources.

### Can I apply to several Italian medical schools at once?
For the visa route, you list one program on Universitaly, and the visa is issued for that course. As a non-EU applicant from abroad, you generally compete only for the seats of that university. Choose based on quotas, fees and your realistic score.

### How much money do I need to show for the Italian student visa?
The consulate requires €10,179.85 per academic year, plus money for a return ticket. The funds must be traceable and can come from relatives up to the fourth degree. An unconfirmed scholarship does not count.
